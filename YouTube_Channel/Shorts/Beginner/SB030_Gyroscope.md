## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A simple top — stationary, balanced on its tip. It tips slightly and falls immediately. Crashes. "NOT SPINNING." Then: the same top, spinning rapidly. Released. It does NOT fall. It drifts sideways. "WAIT."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Non-spinning top: held up, released. Gravity wins — falls in 0.3 seconds. Slow motion of the fall. "GRAVITY: 9.8 m/s². RESISTANCE: 0."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:20):** Same top, spinning at 3000 RPM. Released at the same tilt. Instead of falling: it precesses — rotating slowly around the vertical axis. "WHY IS IT NOT FALLING?"
**Beat 3 (0:20–0:28):** Arrows appear: Gravity (down), Angular momentum vector (along spin axis), Torque (pointing outward, perpendicular to both). "GRAVITY IS REDIRECTING THE SPIN, NOT STOPPING IT."
**Beat 4 (0:28–0:36):** The cross product shown visually: perpendicular arrows. Torque × Angular Momentum = Precession. Precession rate shown proportional to these vectors.
**Beat 5 (0:36–0:43):** Spin reduced via slider: RPM drops, precession rate increases, wobble grows. At low RPM: top falls. "SPIN SAVES IT."
**Beat 6 (0:43–0:50):** High RPM: top precessing smoothly. "3000 RPM." Final: "Angular momentum. The physics is real. The magic is the math."

## Physics Concept Teased
A gyroscope doesn't resist gravity — gravity's torque continuously redirects the angular momentum vector sideways instead of downward, producing precession. Why does this happen and not simply falling?
