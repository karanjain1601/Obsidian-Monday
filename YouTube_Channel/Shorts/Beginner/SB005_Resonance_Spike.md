# Short: The Resonance Spike

**Feeds full video:** I Shook This Spring Until It Tore Itself Apart
**Duration:** ~45 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A spring in the center of frame, barely oscillating. An amplitude bar on the right is nearly flat. A frequency meter at the bottom reads "0.5 Hz" and slowly sweeps upward. Nothing interesting is happening — yet.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:12):** Frequency climbs: 0.5 Hz... 0.7 Hz... 0.9 Hz. Amplitude bar grows very slightly. Spring barely visibly larger oscillations. "NOTHING INTERESTING."
**Beat 2 (0:12–0:20):** 0.95 Hz. A noticeable jump in amplitude. 0.98 Hz. Larger. The amplitude bar is now at 30% of the screen. Viewer begins to sense something building.
**Beat 3 (0:20–0:26):** Counter ticks toward 1.00 Hz. The tension is musical. 0.99 Hz — amplitude bar at 60%. The spring is visibly wild now.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:30):** Frequency hits exactly 1.00 Hz. In a single frame: the amplitude bar SLAMS to the top of the screen, off the chart. The spring is a blur. "RESONANCE." appears in large, pulsing text.
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:38):** Slow motion of the peak moment. The spring stretching and compressing faster than it can recover. Coils becoming a solid blur. Energy pouring in. The spring material begins to glow red.
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** SNAP. Spring tears apart. Fragments flying in slow motion. Silence. Then a single fragment drifts past. Text: "1.00 Hz. WHY THAT FREQUENCY?"

## Physics Concept Teased
What is special about exactly one frequency — the natural frequency — that causes a driven oscillator to absorb infinite energy and tear itself apart?

## Audio
A single driving tone that slowly rises in pitch as the frequency sweeps. Quiet at first — almost inaudible. At 0.95 Hz: the tone becomes audible. At 1.00 Hz: the tone suddenly overwhelms, a resonant chord that is almost painful, then cuts to silence on the snap. Fragment debris: light tinkling sounds.
