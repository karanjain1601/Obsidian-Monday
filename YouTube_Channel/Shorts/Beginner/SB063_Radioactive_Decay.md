# Short: Half-Life Clock

**Feeds full video:** Radioactive Decay: Why Half-Life Is Exactly Half
**Duration:** ~45 seconds

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
1,024 colored atoms arranged in a grid. Every atom glowing uniformly. Timer: 0. Then, randomly — pop, pop, pop — atoms start changing color (decaying). Random which ones. But the count is tracked.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** At t = 0: 1,024 atoms. Time passes. At t = T½: EXACTLY 512 have decayed. "T½: 512 REMAIN. EXACTLY HALF." The remaining ones: indistinguishable from the decayed ones before decay.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Continue. At t = 2T½: 256 remain. At t = 3T½: 128. At t = 4T½: 64. The exponential decay curve being traced in real time alongside the grid. "SAME FRACTION EACH PERIOD."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** "WHY IS IT ALWAYS EXACTLY HALF?" Each atom has a fixed probability p of decaying per unit time. With 1000 atoms: statistically very close to 500 will decay in T½. With 1 atom: exactly 50% chance. "PROBABILITY, NOT SCHEDULE."
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** The decay is memoryless: an atom that has survived 10 half-lives has the same probability of decaying as a fresh atom. "IT DOESN'T REMEMBER. IT DOESN'T AGE."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** The exponential curve: "N(t) = N₀ × (½)^(t/T½) = N₀ × e^(-λt)." The half-life formula shown with λ = ln(2)/T½.
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** Final grid: just a few atoms remaining. The exponential curve approaching (never reaching) zero. "RADIOACTIVE DECAY NEVER FULLY STOPS. IT JUST APPROACHES ZERO." Final: "PROBABILITY GOVERNS THE UNIVERSE."

## Physics Concept Teased
Radioactive decay is fundamentally probabilistic — each atom has a fixed decay probability per unit time, and with large numbers, statistics guarantee that roughly half decay in each half-life, producing the exponential decay law.

## Audio
Each atomic decay: a soft pop. At t=0: nearly silence. As time progresses: pops become a chorus, then slow down as fewer atoms remain. The rhythm of the pops perfectly represents the exponential decay rate — dense at first, sparse at the end.

## Production Notes
Aspect ratio: 9:16. Frame rate: 60fps. Atom grid: 32×32 = 1024 atoms. Undecayed: cyan/bright. Decayed: dark gray. Exponential decay curve plotted in real time alongside the grid. Half-life markers shown on the time axis. The randomness must be genuine — different runs produce slightly different patterns but always converge to the exponential curve statistically.
