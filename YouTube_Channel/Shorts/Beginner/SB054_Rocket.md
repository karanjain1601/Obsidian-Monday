# Short: Rocket in Space

**Feeds full video:** How Rockets Work in the Vacuum of Space
**Duration:** ~45 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A rocket floating in the void of space — no atmosphere, no ground, nothing to push against. An engine plume appears at the bottom. The rocket accelerates. "BUT THERE'S NOTHING TO PUSH AGAINST."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** The rocket expels a stream of exhaust gas downward. Newton's Third Law arrows shown: exhaust momentum (down) and rocket momentum (up) — equal and opposite. "ACTION: GAS DOWN. REACTION: ROCKET UP."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Mass counter shown: rocket + fuel = 10,000 kg, decreasing as fuel burns. Velocity counter: rising. "AS MASS DECREASES, VELOCITY INCREASES."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Tsiolkovsky rocket equation displayed: "Δv = v_e × ln(m₀/m_f)." Variables labeled: exhaust velocity (v_e), initial mass (m₀), final mass (m_f). The equation shows Δv depends on the mass RATIO, not the total mass.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:34):** Two rockets compared: both with 9000 kg of fuel. One: starts with 10,000 kg total (90% fuel). Other: starts with 100,000 kg total (9% fuel). Same fuel, completely different Δv. Mass ratio matters.
**Beat 5 (0:34–0:40):** Fuel exhausted. Rocket: no more thrust. But still moving at high velocity. "NO FUEL LEFT. STILL GOING." The inertia maintains the velocity forever in space.
**Beat 6 (0:40–0:45):** "In space, there's nothing to brake against either. The rocket equation is all you have." Final: "Δv = v_e × ln(m₀/m_f). THAT'S IT."

## Physics Concept Teased
Rockets work by conservation of momentum — expelling mass backward creates equal-and-opposite momentum forward. The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation quantifies how much velocity can be gained from burning fuel based purely on the mass ratio.
