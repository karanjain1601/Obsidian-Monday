# Short: The Siren Passes

**Feeds full video:** Why a Siren's Pitch Changes

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
An ambulance moving from left to right at speed. Sound wavefronts shown as concentric circles — but they're NOT centered on the ambulance. Ahead: circles bunched together. Behind: circles stretched apart. "HEAR THE DIFFERENCE?"

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** Ambulance at rest: perfect concentric circles equally spaced in all directions. Observer hears 440 Hz in all directions. "AT REST: SAME FREQUENCY EVERYWHERE."
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:16):** Ambulance moving right at 30 m/s. Wavefronts bunch ahead (shorter wavelength → higher frequency) and stretch behind (longer wavelength → lower frequency). Both effects shown simultaneously.
**Beat 3 (0:16–0:24):** Observer at bottom of frame: before the ambulance passes, they hear the bunched waves — high pitch. After it passes, they hear the stretched waves — low pitch. The transition happens as the ambulance passes directly in front.
**Beat 4 (0:24–0:30):** Frequency graph shown over time for the observer: flat high frequency approaching, then a rapid drop at the pass-by, then flat lower frequency receding. "THE DOPPLER CURVE."
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** Formula shown: "f_observed = f_source × (v_sound ± v_observer)/(v_sound ∓ v_source)." The plus for approaching, minus for receding.
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** "The siren didn't change. You changed relative to it." Final: "DOPPLER EFFECT. RELATIVE MOTION CHANGES FREQUENCY."

## Physics Concept Teased
The Doppler effect occurs because a moving sound source compresses wavefronts ahead (raising frequency) and stretches them behind (lowering frequency) — the source's pitch hasn't changed, only the wavefront geometry has.
