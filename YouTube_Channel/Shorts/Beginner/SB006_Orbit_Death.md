# Short: The Orbit That Died

**Feeds full video:** I Coded Gravity and My Planet Crashed Into the Sun
**Duration:** ~50 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A glowing blue planet tracing a perfect circular orbit around a golden sun. The orbital trail is crisp and closed. It is beautiful, stable, eternal-looking. Text appears: "YEAR 0. LOOKS FINE."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:12):** Normal orbit speed runs for 2 real seconds. Then time-lapse activates. Year counter starts ticking. Year 50: orbit still perfectly circular, trail stacking cleanly. Year 100: still fine. Text: "STILL FINE."
**Beat 2 (0:12–0:20):** Year 200. A tiny inward drift is barely visible — the circular trail is now slightly oval, the inner edge fractionally closer to the sun. The planet hasn't noticed.
**Beat 3 (0:20–0:28):** Year 500. The drift is unmistakable. The orbit is now clearly an inward spiral. Each new loop slightly tighter. Year counter accelerating.
**Beat 4 (0:28–0:36):** Year 800. The spiral is tight, dramatic, alarming. The planet visibly accelerating as it falls inward. Trail lines stacking into a dense coil toward the sun.
**Beat 5 (0:36–0:43):** Year 1,247. The planet hits the sun. Bright white flash. Shockwave ring expands outward. Year counter stops. The sun pulses brighter.
**Beat 6 (0:43–0:50):** Empty space where the orbit used to be. "REAL PLANETS DON'T DO THIS." Long pause. "SO WHY DID MINE?"

## Physics Concept Teased
Numerical integration in a gravity simulation bleeds energy from the orbit with each timestep — a planet that should orbit forever slowly spirals into the sun because the simulation itself introduces friction that doesn't exist in nature.

## On-Screen Text / Captions
- "YEAR 0. LOOKS FINE." (opening)
- Year counter: "YEAR 50... 100... 200... 500... 800... 1,247" (ticking)
- "STILL FINE." (Year 100, reassuring — too reassuring)
- "YEAR 1,247: IMPACT" (flash frame)
- "REAL PLANETS DON'T DO THIS."
- "SO WHY DID MINE?" (final hold)

## Audio
Gentle, sweeping ambient space music during the stable orbit. Subtle urgency enters at the first spiral. Music swells dramatically as the spiral tightens. Silence in the last second before impact. Impact: deep orchestral hit. Then silence — the music that was playing is gone too, as if erased with the planet.

## Production Notes
Aspect ratio: 9:16. Frame rate: 60fps at normal speed, time-lapse effect via trail opacity stacking. Sun at center: golden (#FDD835) with soft glow. Planet: cyan (#29B6F6) with a small glow. Orbital trail: same cyan, opacity fading with age. Year counter in monospace top-left corner. Trail accumulation makes the spiral visible and dramatic. The flash at impact should briefly wash the entire frame white.
