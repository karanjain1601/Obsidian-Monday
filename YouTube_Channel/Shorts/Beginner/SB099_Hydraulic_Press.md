# Short: Pascal Amplifier

**Feeds full video:** The Hydraulic Press: How a Small Force Lifts a Car
**Duration:** ~40 seconds

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A small piston (area = 1 cm²) pushed down with one finger. A large piston (area = 100 cm²) on the other side lifts a car. "ONE FINGER. ONE CAR." The lever of the 21st century.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** Pascal's principle: "PRESSURE IS TRANSMITTED EQUALLY IN ALL DIRECTIONS IN A FLUID." Small piston: force F₁ = 100 N, area A₁ = 1 cm². Pressure P = F₁/A₁ = 100 N/cm².
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:16):** Same pressure reaches the large piston (A₂ = 100 cm²). Force: F₂ = P × A₂ = 100 × 100 = 10,000 N. "F₂ = F₁ × (A₂/A₁). THE FORCE IS MULTIPLIED."
**Beat 3 (0:16–0:24):** The car is lifted. The force amplification is 100×. But — the large piston moves only 1/100 of the distance the small piston moves. "FORCE AMPLIFIED. DISTANCE REDUCED. ENERGY CONSERVED."
**Beat 4 (0:24–0:30):** Work done: "W = F₁ × d₁ = F₂ × d₂. BOTH EQUAL." No energy is created — it's just redistributed. Pascal's principle doesn't violate energy conservation.
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** Applications shown: hydraulic car jack, hydraulic press (crushing a car), brake system (foot force → wheel force). "EVERY HYDRAULIC MACHINE: THE SAME PRINCIPLE."
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** "Blaise Pascal discovered this in 1653. It took until 1795 for Joseph Bramah to build the first hydraulic press." Final: "PASCAL'S PRINCIPLE. FORCE × AREA."

## Physics Concept Teased
Pascal's principle states that pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted undiminished in all directions — allowing a small force on a small piston to generate a large force on a large piston, with no violation of energy conservation.
