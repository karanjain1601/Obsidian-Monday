# Short: Perfect Hourglass

**Feeds full video:** Sand Is Not a Liquid and Not a Solid

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
An hourglass. Classic shape. Top chamber full of golden sand. It tips. Sand begins to flow — but not like liquid. Falls in a column, pile below building with that characteristic angle.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Top chamber: sand pile collapsing toward the neck. Individual grains visible tumbling down the slope. The slope angle is consistent — the angle of repose.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** The neck: grains funneling through, forming a steady stream. Close-up shows grains momentarily arching across the neck (jamming), then releasing. Flow is intermittent, not continuous.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:28):** Bottom chamber: grains landing on growing pile. Each grain bouncing once, then settling. Perfect cone at angle of repose (~34°). Angle marker shown.
**Beat 4 (0:28–0:36):** Close-up: two grains falling simultaneously — they collide, redirect, and both come to rest. 10,000 independent contacts per second.
**Beat 5 (0:36–0:43):** Full hourglass with upper pile draining and lower pile growing. Beautiful symmetry. "NOT A LIQUID. NOT A SOLID."
**Beat 6 (0:43–0:50):** Pause mid-pour. Zoom on individual grains: irregular shapes, frictional contacts, micro-scale forces. "EACH GRAIN. ITS OWN PHYSICS. 10,000 OF THEM."

## Physics Concept Teased
Sand exhibits properties of neither liquids nor solids — it can flow, pile, jam, arch, and support weight, all from pure contact forces between irregular particles. What is it?
