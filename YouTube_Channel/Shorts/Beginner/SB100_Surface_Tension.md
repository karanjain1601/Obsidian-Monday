## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A water drop landing on a waxy leaf. Instead of spreading: it forms a perfect sphere. Contact angle shown: 150°. The drop is nearly a complete ball. It wobbles. It rolls. It is magic. And it is physics.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Molecular view: water molecules at the surface have fewer neighbors than interior molecules. Each surface molecule is pulled inward — net force pointing toward the interior. "SURFACE TENSION: UNBALANCED INTERMOLECULAR FORCES."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** The water drop on a waxy surface: contact angle shown (~150°, superhydrophobic). The drop is a nearly perfect sphere because the wax gives no "purchase" for water to wet it. Lotus effect: tiny waxy bumps keeping the drop elevated.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Surface tension energy: γ = 0.072 N/m for water. The drop minimizes its surface area — hence the spherical shape. "MINIMUM SURFACE AREA = MINIMUM ENERGY = SPHERE."
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** A water strider on the water surface. Close-up of the dimples made by its feet — the water surface is dented but not broken. Surface tension supports the insect's weight. "THE SURFACE IS AN ELASTIC MEMBRANE."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** The force balance: weight of the insect (down) equals surface tension force along the perimeter of contact (up). Surface tension force = γ × 2L (two sides of each leg). Larger insects can't walk on water — they exceed the maximum surface tension force.
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** Needle placed on water: it floats despite being denser than water — surface tension supports it. "WATER IS HELD UP BY ITS OWN SKIN." Final: "SURFACE TENSION. THE SKIN OF WATER."

## Physics Concept Teased
Surface tension arises from unbalanced intermolecular forces at the water surface — molecules there are pulled inward with no outward partner — creating an effective elastic skin that can support insects, cause water to bead, and allow needles to float.
