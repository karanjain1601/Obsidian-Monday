# Short: Vacuum Drop

**Feeds full video:** I Coded Free Fall. Then I Added Air.
**Duration:** ~40 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Split-screen. Left: "VACUUM." Right: "AIR." Two objects at the top of each frame: a feather and a cannonball. They drop simultaneously. In the vacuum — they fall together perfectly.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Vacuum side: feather and cannonball fall in perfect sync. Same acceleration, same position at every frame. "g = 9.8 m/s². BOTH." Speed counter: identical for both.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:20):** Air side: cannonball accelerates away — very slight drag effect, falls nearly as fast. Feather: barely moving, drifting slowly, swaying, catching air. The gap between them grows rapidly.
**Beat 3 (0:20–0:28):** At 2 seconds: air side — cannonball near the bottom, feather only 20% of the way down. Vacuum side: both objects hit the bottom simultaneously. Slow motion on both impacts.
**Beat 4 (0:28–0:35):** Drag force arrows shown on each object: feather has an enormous upward drag arrow (relative to weight). Cannonball: tiny upward drag arrow (negligible). The drag-to-weight ratio is all that matters.
**Beat 5 (0:35–0:40):** "In 1971, astronaut David Scott dropped a hammer and feather on the Moon. Both hit at the same time." Final: "NO AIR. NO DIFFERENCE."

## Physics Concept Teased
Air resistance depends on size, shape, and velocity — but gravity is the same for all objects. Without air, the feather and cannonball are identical in free fall. With air, their drag-to-weight ratio determines everything.

## Audio
Vacuum side: complete silence — the absence of air medium also means no sound. Air side: a light ambient hum and the feather's gentle flutter sound. Both impacts: vacuum side — a clean impact sound; air side — the cannonball lands and the feather drifts down seconds later with a soft touch.

## Production Notes
Aspect ratio: 9:16. Frame rate: 60fps. Perfect 50/50 split. Objects: feather (realistic, light) and cannonball (dark, spherical). Vacuum background: flat black. Air background: slight blue tint (sky-like). Drag arrows: red upward vectors, length proportional to drag force. The feather on the air side should sway and flutter realistically. Impact timing difference should be obvious — don't make it subtle.
