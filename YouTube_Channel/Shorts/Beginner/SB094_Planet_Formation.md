# Short: Dust Accretes

**Feeds full video:** How Planets Form From a Disk of Dust

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A flat disk of dust and gas — a protoplanetary disk seen edge-on, then top-down. Millions of tiny particles swirling around a central protostar. "4.6 BILLION YEARS AGO. THIS WAS THE SOLAR SYSTEM."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:12):** Time-lapse begins. Dust particles colliding, sticking. Small clumps forming — planetesimals. The clumps are irregular, rocky. "COLLISION + STICKING = GROWTH."
**Beat 2 (0:12–0:22):** Larger clumps sweeping up smaller ones. The gravitational cross-section of each growing body increases — it can attract dust from a wider region. "GRAVITY HELPS GRAVITY GROW."
**Beat 3 (0:22–0:32):** Proto-planets appear — 10 to 50 of them at various sizes. They interact gravitationally, sometimes merging catastrophically, sometimes ejecting each other. Orbital chaos visible.
**Beat 4 (0:32–0:40):** Orbital clearing: the larger proto-planets sweep up or eject all other bodies in their orbital lane. "CLEARING THE NEIGHBORHOOD." A few dominant planets remain. Gaps appearing in the disk.
**Beat 5 (0:40–0:46):** The final solar system: 8 (or so) planets, clear orbital gaps, the disk dispersed. "1 MILLION YEARS OF CHAOS. 4.6 BILLION YEARS OF STABILITY."
**Beat 6 (0:46–0:50):** "Every grain of sand on Earth was once floating in that disk." Final: "ACCRETION. THE UNIVERSE MAKING PLANETS FROM NOTHING BUT TIME AND GRAVITY."

## Physics Concept Teased
Planetary formation begins with random dust collisions and electrostatic sticking, then gravitational accretion takes over — each growing body increases its gravitational capture cross-section, creating runaway growth that clears orbital lanes.
