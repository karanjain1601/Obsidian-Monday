# Short: Wobbling Table

**Feeds full video:** The Tipping Point: Why Tables Fall Over
**Duration:** ~40 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A table on flat ground. A vertical dotted line drops from the center of mass downward. It falls exactly between the four legs. The table is stable. Then one leg steps off a curb.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** Normal table: center of mass (CM) projection shown as a downward line landing inside the support polygon (the quadrilateral formed by the four legs). "CM INSIDE BASE: STABLE."
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:16):** One front leg steps off a step — the table tilts. CM projection shifts. It's still inside the support polygon (now a triangle of three legs). "STILL STABLE — BARELY."
**Beat 3 (0:16–0:22):** The step gets higher. The tilt increases. CM projection shifts further. It reaches the edge of the support polygon. "AT THE LIMIT."
**Beat 4 (0:22–0:28):** One more degree of tilt. CM projection crosses outside the support polygon. Immediately: the table tips. Gravity now creates a torque that rotates it further — a positive feedback. Crash.
**Beat 5 (0:28–0:34):** Slow motion of the tipping. The torque arrow shown: when CM is outside the base, the torque acts to tip it further (unstable). When CM is inside, the torque acts to restore it (stable).
**Beat 6 (0:34–0:40):** "Tipping is about where the center of mass is, not how much it weighs." Tall narrow table vs short wide table comparison. "SAME WEIGHT. ONE TIPS EASILY." Final: "CENTER OF MASS DECIDES STABILITY."

## Physics Concept Teased
A table (or any object) is stable as long as its center of mass projects vertically within its support polygon — the moment it crosses the polygon's edge, gravity produces a torque that tips it further rather than restoring it.
