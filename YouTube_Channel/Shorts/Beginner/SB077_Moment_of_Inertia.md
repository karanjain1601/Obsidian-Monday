# Short: Ball vs Ring Race

**Feeds full video:** Why a Ball Rolls Slower Than It Slides"

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A ramp. At the top: a solid ball and a hollow ring, side by side. They look different in shape but are the same mass and same size. "SAME MASS. SAME SIZE. ONE WILL WIN." They release simultaneously.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Both objects roll down the ramp. The solid ball is ahead after 1 second. After 2 seconds: significantly ahead. They reach the bottom — solid ball wins by a clear margin. "SOLID BALL WINS. EVERY TIME."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** "WHY?" Energy diagram. Total energy = gravitational PE. At the bottom: split between translational KE and rotational KE. "SOME ENERGY IS 'STUCK' IN SPINNING."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Moment of inertia shown: "SOLID BALL: I = (2/5)mr²." "HOLLOW RING: I = mr²." The ring has a higher I because its mass is distributed at the maximum possible radius.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** Energy split: ring must put MORE of its PE into rotational KE (high I means it spins fast to keep up). Less left for translational motion. Ball: lower I, more energy goes to translation. "THE RING SPINS MORE. MOVES LESS."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:37):** Comparison: if both SLID down (no rolling, frictionless): they tie. Rolling penalizes objects with more distributed mass. "SLIDING BALL vs ROLLING BALL: SLIDER WINS."
**Beat 6 (0:37–0:40):** "The shape of an object determines how fast it rolls down a ramp. Nothing else." Final: "I = (2/5)mr² vs I = mr². SHAPE DECIDES THE RACE."

## Physics Concept Teased
Rolling objects must split their gravitational potential energy between translational and rotational kinetic energy — objects with higher moments of inertia (mass concentrated at large radius) are penalized more and roll slower.
