# Short: Boomerang Returns

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A boomerang thrown in what looks like a straight line. Top-down view. It begins curving — slowly at first, then more tightly. And it returns to the thrower. "WHY DOES IT COME BACK?"

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** The boomerang in flight, spinning. Each blade is an airfoil — generating lift. But the top blade moves faster through the air (same spin + forward motion) than the bottom blade (spin − forward motion). "ASYMMETRIC LIFT."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Higher lift at the top → a rolling torque acts on the boomerang. But the boomerang is spinning — it's a gyroscope. A torque on a gyroscope causes PRECESSION, not rolling. The precession bends the flight path sideways.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Vector diagram: lift force (up), gyroscopic angular momentum (along spin axis), precession torque (bending the flight path). The precession continuously curves the path in a circle.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** Top-down path of the boomerang: a roughly circular arc returning to the thrower. The path is curved continuously by the precession effect — every point of the trajectory is slightly curved.
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** Comparison: a non-spinning airfoil thrown the same way flies straight and doesn't return. The spin is essential. "SPIN MAKES IT A GYROSCOPE. THE GYROSCOPE MAKES IT RETURN."
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** Boomerang completes its arc and returns. "ASYMMETRIC LIFT → GYROSCOPIC PRECESSION → CIRCULAR PATH." Final: "THE BOOMERANG DOESN'T KNOW WHERE YOU ARE. IT JUST FOLLOWS THE PHYSICS."

## Physics Concept Teased
A boomerang returns because asymmetric lift (top blade moves faster than bottom blade) creates a torque on the spinning airfoil — but because it's spinning (a gyroscope), this torque causes precession rather than rolling, continuously curving the flight path in a circle.

## Audio
The boomerang's spin creates a characteristic humming sound that changes in pitch as the flight progresses (Doppler effect — it moves away then returns). The return: the pitch rises as it approaches. Classic boomerang sound effect.
