# Short: Beat Frequency

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Two sine waves on screen. Both look nearly identical. Their sum: a third wave below — but this third wave's amplitude is pulsing, oscillating slowly. "NEITHER WAVE DOES THIS. TOGETHER THEY DO."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** Wave 1: "f₁ = 440 Hz." Clean sine wave. Wave 2: "f₂ = 443 Hz." Almost identical. Individually shown: both look the same to the eye.
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:16):** Sum wave shown. The amplitude oscillates: loud → quiet → loud → quiet → loud. Beat frequency shown: "f_beat = |f₁ - f₂| = 3 Hz." 3 pulses per second visible.
**Beat 3 (0:16–0:24):** f₂ slider moved: f₂ = 450 Hz. Beat frequency increases: 10 Hz. The pulsing gets much faster. f₂ = 460 Hz: 20 Hz beats — a rapid tremolo.
**Beat 4 (0:24–0:30):** f₂ moved far away: 480 Hz. The beats are so fast they blur into a different pitch — we hear a roughness, not individual beats. "TOO FAST TO HEAR AS BEATS."
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** Back to f₂ = 441 Hz: slow, single beat per second. "THIS IS HOW PIANO TUNERS TUNE BY EAR." When beats disappear: the strings are in unison. A piano tuner animation.
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** "3 Hz difference. 3 beats per second. Exactly." Final: "BEAT FREQUENCY. THE PHYSICS OF DISSONANCE."

## Physics Concept Teased
When two frequencies close together are added, their sum produces a slow amplitude oscillation at the difference frequency — the beat — because the two waves alternately reinforce and cancel each other as their relative phase cycles.
