# Short: 45° Is Wrong

**Feeds full video:** Why Bullets Don't Go As Far As Physics Class Says
**Duration:** ~45 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A cannon at 45°. A perfect golden parabola arcs to a target 100m away. The label "TEXTBOOK" appears. Then a buzzer sound. Then the arc shrinks.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Textbook launch: cannon fires at 45°, ball traces a perfect parabola, lands at the "100m" marker. Label: "NO AIR. TEXTBOOK PHYSICS." Clean, satisfying.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:20):** "NOW ADD AIR." Same cannon, same 45°, same speed. Ball launches — but the arc is visibly different: shorter, steeper on the way down. Lands at the "61m" marker. Label: "REAL WORLD."
**Beat 3 (0:20–0:30):** OVERLAY both arcs. The textbook arc is twice as long and high. Distance markers show the gap: 100m vs 61m. "DRAG REDUCED RANGE BY 39%." Arrow shows the gap.
**Beat 4 (0:30–0:38):** "BUT WAIT. 45° STILL OPTIMAL WITH DRAG?" New arc: 38°. It goes FARTHER than 45° with drag. Lands past the 61m marker. Both 45° and 38° arcs shown together with drag: 38° wins.
**Beat 5 (0:38–0:43):** Textbook: "OPTIMAL: 45°." Reality: "OPTIMAL: ~38° (depends on drag coefficient)." Side by side labels.
**Beat 6 (0:43–0:45):** "Physics class lied. A little." Final frame.

## Physics Concept Teased
With drag proportional to velocity squared, the optimal launch angle for maximum range is less than 45° and depends on the object's drag coefficient — why does air resistance shift the optimal angle?
