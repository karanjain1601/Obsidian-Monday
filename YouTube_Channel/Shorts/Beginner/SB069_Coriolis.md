## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Earth from above — the North Pole. A low-pressure center appears. Arrows show wind rushing inward from all sides — but curving. In the Northern Hemisphere: curving RIGHT, creating a counterclockwise spiral. "WHY DOES IT CURVE?"

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** North Pole view. Air rushing toward low-pressure center. The Coriolis force deflects it to the right — arrows curve rightward before reaching the center. "CORIOLIS FORCE: DEFLECTS RIGHT IN NH."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** The counterclockwise spiral forms as all the right-deflected air spirals inward and counterclockwise. Classic hurricane shape from above. "NORTHERN HEMISPHERE: COUNTERCLOCKWISE."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:25):** Switch to Southern Hemisphere: same low pressure, same air rushing in. Coriolis force here deflects LEFT (because the reference frame is opposite). Spiral forms clockwise. "SOUTHERN HEMISPHERE: CLOCKWISE."
**Beat 4 (0:25–0:32):** Side-by-side: NH hurricane (counterclockwise) vs SH cyclone (clockwise). Same physics — opposite rotations. "SAME EQUATIONS. OPPOSITE SPIN." Equator: no Coriolis effect — storms don't form there.
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:37):** Coriolis force formula: "F_c = -2mΩ × v." At the equator: Ω component is horizontal — no deflection. At the poles: maximum deflection.
**Beat 6 (0:37–0:40):** "The Earth's rotation makes every storm on Earth spin. And it knows which hemisphere it's in." Final: "CORIOLIS FORCE."

## Physics Concept Teased
The Coriolis effect arises because Earth rotates under moving air — deflecting it to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and left in the Southern Hemisphere — causing all large-scale atmospheric circulation to spiral.
