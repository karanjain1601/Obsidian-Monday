# Short: Satellite Falls Sideways

**Feeds full video:** Why Satellites Don't Fall

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Earth. A ball thrown horizontally from a very tall tower. At 1 km/s: it falls in a parabola, hits the ground. At 3 km/s: hits farther. At 7.9 km/s: the trajectory curves — but Earth curves at the same rate. Orbit.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Series of projectile throws at increasing horizontal speeds. Each trajectory shown arcing downward. The Earth curving away underneath. At 7.9 km/s: "THE GROUND CURVES AWAY AS FAST AS IT FALLS."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** The orbital condition shown geometrically: free-fall acceleration downward (g = 9.8 m/s²) exactly matches the curvature of Earth (which "drops" 5m for every 8km of horizontal travel). "FALLING AND MISSING."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Satellite orbit at 7.9 km/s: a closed circle. The satellite IS falling — force arrow shows gravity pulling it down. But it's going sideways fast enough that the Earth keeps curving away below it.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** Show what happens if the satellite is slowed: the orbit decays — the satellite falls in a spiral toward Earth. Speed up: the orbit rises. "SPEED DETERMINES ALTITUDE."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:37):** ISS: 7.66 km/s at 400 km altitude. Counter shown. "THE ISS IS IN CONSTANT FREE-FALL. THE ASTRONAUTS TOO."
**Beat 6 (0:37–0:40):** "Satellites don't fight gravity. They use it." Final: "ORBIT IS JUST FALLING WITH STYLE."

## Physics Concept Teased
A satellite orbits by moving so fast horizontally that Earth's surface curves away at the same rate as the satellite falls — it is in continuous free-fall, never hitting Earth because Earth keeps getting out of the way.
