## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A trebuchet. Massive counterweight on one side, long throwing arm, a sling holding a projectile. The counterweight drops. The arm swings in a beautiful arc. The sling extends. And at the perfect moment — release. Projectile soars.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** The counterweight drops: gravitational PE converts to kinetic energy. The arm swings — rotational energy increasing. Energy flow diagram shown: "GPE → Rotational KE → Projectile KE."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** The sling extends at the top of the arc. This mechanical advantage: the sling effectively extends the arm length at the crucial moment, adding to the projectile velocity. "THE SLING DOUBLES THE ARM LENGTH."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** The release angle optimization: if released too early → angle too high → short range. Too late → angle too low → hits the ground close. The optimal release: ~45° (approximately) for maximum range. Release point shown.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** Three comparison launches: early release (angle too high), optimal (correct angle, flies farthest), late release (angle too low). Three arcs shown simultaneously.
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** Parameter optimization: arm length ratio, counterweight mass ratio, sling length — each slider changed, range plotted. Gradient descent shown finding the optimal design.
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** Optimal trebuchet fires. Projectile soars in a perfect arc. "MEDIEVAL ENGINEERS OPTIMIZED THIS FOR CENTURIES. CODE DID IT IN 0.2 SECONDS." Final: "ENERGY. LEVERAGE. TIMING."

## Physics Concept Teased
A trebuchet converts counterweight gravitational potential energy into projectile kinetic energy through a rotating arm and sling — but the release timing and mechanical ratios must be carefully optimized to maximize range.
