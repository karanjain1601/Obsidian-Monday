# Short: Raindrop Rainbow

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A single spherical water droplet, illuminated by white light from the Sun behind and to the left. Light enters the droplet. Something happens inside. And then: rays of different colors exit at different angles. A rainbow in one raindrop.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** The ray entering the droplet: it refracts at the surface, bending according to Snell's law. Different wavelengths (colors) bend at different angles — dispersion. "SNELL'S LAW: DIFFERENT COLORS BEND DIFFERENTLY."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Inside the droplet: the ray reflects off the back curved surface (internal reflection). Then exits the front, refracting again. Total deviation angle shown: red exits at ~42°, violet exits at ~40°.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:25):** "THE RAINBOW ANGLE." The observer's eye receives red from drops at 42° above the anti-solar point, violet from drops at 40°. Different drops, different colors, one rainbow.
**Beat 4 (0:25–0:32):** Multiple droplets shown: each droplet sends a different color to the observer. Red from high drops. Violet from low drops. The full arc appears. "MILLIONS OF DROPLETS. ONE ARC."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** The secondary rainbow: two internal reflections. Colors reversed (red inside, violet outside). Darker sky between primary and secondary: Alexander's dark band. "THE SECOND RAINBOW REVERSES THE ORDER."
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** "Every raindrop makes a rainbow. You just have to be in the right place." The geometry: Sun behind you, rain in front, 42° up. Final: "GEOMETRY AND DISPERSION. THE PHYSICS OF WONDER."

## Physics Concept Teased
A rainbow requires dispersion (different wavelengths refract at different angles in water) and the specific geometry of a spherical droplet (refraction + internal reflection + refraction) — red exits at 42°, violet at 40°, placing them at specific angular positions above the antisolar point.
