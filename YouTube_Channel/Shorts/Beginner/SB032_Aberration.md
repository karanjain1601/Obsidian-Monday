# Short: Stars Rush to the Front

**Feeds full video:** What Does 99% Light Speed Actually Look Like?
**Duration:** ~45 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A normal starfield. Stars evenly distributed in all directions around the viewer. Velocity: 0. The scene is familiar, peaceful, a night sky in space. Then the velocity slider begins to move.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Velocity: 0.30c. The stars ahead very slightly brighten and blueshift. Stars behind redden and dim. Subtle but present.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Velocity: 0.70c. Stars are clearly migrating toward the forward direction — the star density in front visibly higher. Stars behind: sparse and red. "ABERRATION."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Velocity: 0.90c. The forward hemisphere is now packed with stars, all blueshifted toward white. The rear hemisphere is almost empty. The universe is collecting in front of the spacecraft.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:34):** Velocity: 0.99c. All stars compressed into a blazing circular disk of blue-white light ahead — like a searchlight from infinity. Behind: complete darkness. "ALL STARS. IN ONE DIRECTION."
**Beat 5 (0:34–0:40):** Velocity slider slowly drops back to 0. Stars migrate back outward, returning to their isotropic distribution. The universe "uncollects."
**Beat 6 (0:40–0:45):** Static at 0.99c: the blazing forward disk. "At 99% light speed, the entire visible universe is compressed into 8.1°." Final: "THE UNIVERSE BENDS AROUND SPEED."

## Physics Concept Teased
Relativistic aberration causes a moving observer to see stars displaced toward the forward direction — and at high speeds, the entire visible universe collapses into a blazing point ahead, with darkness behind.

## Production Notes
Aspect ratio: 9:16. Frame rate: 60fps. Starfield: 10,000 stars with correct spectral colors. Aberration computed correctly per special relativity. Doppler shift changes star colors: blue ahead, red behind. Brightness increases ahead (beaming effect). At 0.99c, forward aberration half-angle is ~8.1° — must be visually apparent as a very tight disk. Camera is first-person, immersive.
