# Short: Mercury's Orbit Drifts

**Feeds full video:** Planets Near a Black Hole Don't Follow Newton
**Duration:** ~45 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Mercury in a close elliptical orbit around the Sun. The orbit is traced and appears closed. Text: "NEWTON'S GRAVITY." The orbit repeats perfectly — ellipse on top of ellipse.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Newtonian gravity: 10 orbits traced. Perfect closed ellipse — each orbit exactly atop the last. "NEWTON SAYS: CLOSED ELLIPSE." Perihelion marker stays fixed.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Switch: "GENERAL RELATIVITY." Reset. Mercury begins orbiting. After one orbit: perihelion is in a slightly different position. After 5 orbits: the shift is visible.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** After 50 orbits in GR: the perihelion has moved noticeably — the ellipse has precessed. After 100 orbits: the axis has rotated by a visible angle. A rosette pattern forming.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:34):** Split-screen: Newtonian (left, fixed ellipse) vs GR (right, rotating rosette). "NEWTON COULDN'T EXPLAIN THIS." Precession rate shown: "43 arcseconds per century."
**Beat 5 (0:34–0:40):** The 43 arcsec/century annotation on the rosette. "43 arcseconds. That's 0.012 degrees per century." Yet the error accumulated to a clearly wrong prediction over 200 years of observation.
**Beat 6 (0:40–0:45):** "Newton's gravity was wrong by 0.012 degrees per century. Einstein's fixed it." Final: "EINSTEIN GOT IT RIGHT."

## Physics Concept Teased
Mercury's orbit precesses 43 arcseconds per century more than Newton's gravity predicts — a tiny effect that stumped astronomers for 60 years until Einstein's general relativity explained it exactly.

## Production Notes
Aspect ratio: 9:16. Frame rate: 60fps. Solar system top-down. Sun: golden glow at center. Mercury: small orange dot. Orbit trails: persist, showing cumulative precession. Perihelion marker: a small arrow that moves as the orbit precesses. The rosette pattern after 100 orbits should be clearly recognizable as such.
