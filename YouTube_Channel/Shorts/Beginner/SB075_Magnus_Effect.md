## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A baseball leaving a pitcher's hand, spinning clockwise (viewed from above). Its trajectory over the plate: clearly curving — not the straight path physics class might suggest. "THE BALL IS SPINNING. THE BALL IS LYING."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Baseball in flight, spinning clockwise. Air streamlines shown around the ball. On one side (spin direction matches airflow): streamlines faster. On the other side (spin opposes airflow): streamlines slower.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Bernoulli pressure map: lower pressure on the fast-streamline side, higher pressure on the slow side. Net pressure force shown: perpendicular to the velocity, toward the low-pressure side. "MAGNUS FORCE."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:25):** The trajectory from the pitcher to home plate: the ball curves ~45 cm (18 inches) from its expected straight-line path. "45 cm CURVE FROM SPIN ALONE."
**Beat 4 (0:25–0:32):** Spin direction comparison: clockwise spin (from above) → curves right. Counterclockwise → curves left. Top-spin → curves down. Back-spin → curves up (reduces gravity drop). "SPIN DIRECTION = CURVE DIRECTION."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:37):** Magnus force formula: "F = ρ × Γ × v." Circulation Γ related to spin rate and ball size. Higher spin rate → stronger Magnus force → tighter curve.
**Beat 6 (0:37–0:40):** "The batter has 0.4 seconds to decide. The ball uses every one of them lying." Final: "MAGNUS FORCE. THE PHYSICS OF DECEPTION."

## Physics Concept Teased
The Magnus effect causes a spinning ball to curve because spin creates asymmetric airflow: faster flow (lower pressure) on one side, slower flow (higher pressure) on the other, producing a net lateral force perpendicular to the ball's velocity.
