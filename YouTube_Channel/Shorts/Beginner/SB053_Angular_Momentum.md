# Short: The Figure Skater

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A figure skater spinning slowly on ice, arms extended wide. RPM counter in corner: "RPM: 60." Moment of inertia displayed: "I = HIGH." Then the arms start to pull in.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Arms fully extended. RPM: 60. Moment of inertia (I) shown as a large blue bar. The skater is spinning slowly, gracefully. Angular momentum (L = I × ω) shown: "L = 100."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Arms begin pulling in. As arms move inward: I bar shrinks. RPM counter begins climbing: 70... 80... 100... 150. "L = 100." (unchanged — conservation of angular momentum).
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:25):** Arms fully tucked. I at minimum. RPM: 240. The skater is spinning rapidly — almost too fast to see clearly. "L = 100." Still exactly 100.
**Beat 4 (0:25–0:32):** "L = I × ω. L is constant." As I shrinks, ω must grow. ω = L/I. Formula shown as an animation: when I is halved, ω doubles. Shown numerically: I/2 → ω×2.
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:37):** Arms extend again. RPM falls back to 60. "L = 100." The cycle shown twice. Perfect, reversible conservation.
**Beat 6 (0:37–0:40):** "No external torque. No energy added. Just physics." Final: "ANGULAR MOMENTUM NEVER LIES."

## Physics Concept Teased
Conservation of angular momentum (L = Iω = constant) means that when a skater pulls their arms in and reduces their moment of inertia, their angular velocity must increase proportionally — no external force required.

## Audio
The skater's spinning produces a sound — the ice scrape and the whoosh of motion — that increases in pitch proportionally with RPM. Arms in: pitch rises dramatically. Arms out: pitch falls. The audio is a direct readout of angular velocity.

## Production Notes
Aspect ratio: 9:16. Frame rate: 60fps. Skater: top-down or slight 3/4 view for clarity. Arm position clearly visible. RPM counter: large, prominent. Moment of inertia visualization: a bar or rings extending from center (arms extended = large ring, arms tucked = small ring). Angular momentum value: constant numerical display. The RPM numbers must actually be correct — if I doubles (arms out vs tucked), RPM must halve.
