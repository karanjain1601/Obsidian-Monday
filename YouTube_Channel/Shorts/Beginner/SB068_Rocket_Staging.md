# Short: Rocket Staging

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A single-stage rocket launches. It burns fuel, accelerates. But as it climbs, it's getting slower to accelerate — it's carrying an enormous empty tank. "SINGLE STAGE. 9 km/s NEEDED. ACHIEVED: 4 km/s. FAILS TO ORBIT."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Single-stage rocket: 90% fuel, 10% payload+structure. Rocket equation: Δv = 2.3 km/s × ln(10) = 5.3 km/s. "NOT ENOUGH FOR ORBIT (9.4 km/s needed)." Failed trajectory shown.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Two-stage rocket launches. Stage 1 burns. "STAGE 1 SEPARATION." The empty first stage drops away — the heavy structure is jettisoned. The remaining rocket is now much lighter.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:25):** Stage 2 ignites. Now starting from rest with a much better mass ratio — no empty first stage. Stage 2 burns and achieves a much higher Δv. "STAGE 2: +4.5 km/s."
**Beat 4 (0:25–0:32):** Total Δv: Stage 1 + Stage 2 > what a single stage could achieve. Mass ratio visualization: single stage vs two-stage comparison showing why the sum exceeds the whole. "THE MATH MAKES STAGING MANDATORY."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** Three-stage rocket: even better. Saturn V had three stages. Each stage: better mass ratio than the whole rocket could achieve in a single stage.
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** Final: mass ratio bars for 1-stage, 2-stage, 3-stage rockets all compared. "STAGING DOESN'T CHEAT THE ROCKET EQUATION. IT APPLIES IT MULTIPLE TIMES." Final: "THAT'S THE TRICK."

## Physics Concept Teased
The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation penalizes carrying dead mass (empty tanks) — by staging, you jettison that dead weight mid-flight, allowing each stage to start with a favorable mass ratio and accumulate Δv that no single stage could achieve.
