## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A helicopter rotor blade cross-section moving through air. The blade is tilted — it's an airfoil. Streamlines show lift being generated. But this is just one section of the blade — at a specific radius. "THE BLADE HAS DIFFERENT SPEEDS EVERYWHERE."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** The rotor blade from above. At the hub (radius = 0): velocity = 0. At mid-radius: velocity = ω × r (moderate). At the tip: velocity = ω × R (maximum). "SPEED VARIES ALONG THE BLADE."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Lift per unit length: proportional to v² at each radius position. Inner sections: low speed, low lift. Outer sections: high speed, high lift. Lift distribution shown as a curve along the blade.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Total lift integrated along the blade: shown as an area under the lift-per-length curve. "TOTAL LIFT = INTEGRAL OF LOCAL LIFT ACROSS THE SPAN."
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:33):** Collective pitch: all blades pitched together (angle of attack increased for all sections). Lift increases uniformly. "COLLECTIVE UP: HELICOPTER CLIMBS."
**Beat 5 (0:33–0:39):** Cyclic pitch: blade pitch changes sinusoidally with rotation angle — one side of the disk pitched more, other side less. This tilts the lift disk, providing directional thrust. "CYCLIC: HELICOPTER TILTS AND MOVES."
**Beat 6 (0:39–0:45):** Helicopter shown in hover with all forces balanced: lift = weight, torque countered by tail rotor. "COLLECTIVE FOR HEIGHT. CYCLIC FOR DIRECTION. TAIL ROTOR FOR SPIN." Final: "THE ROTOR IS A FLYING COMPUTER."

## Physics Concept Teased
Helicopter lift is generated by an airfoil rotating about a central hub — but the blade velocity varies linearly from zero at the hub to maximum at the tip, so lift is distributed non-uniformly and controlled by pitching the blade angle through collective and cyclic controls.

## Audio
The rotor sound: a distinctive whomp-whomp-whomp that changes character with blade pitch. Collective increase: deeper, stronger rotor sound as lift increases. Cyclic change: an asymmetric sound as one side of the disk generates more lift. The audio directly reflects the blade loading.
