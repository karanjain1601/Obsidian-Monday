# Short: Why Planes Fly

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
An airfoil cross-section in a wind tunnel. Streamlines flowing over and under the wing. The streamlines above are visibly more crowded — moving faster. A pressure map appears: blue (high) below, red (low) above. A net upward arrow appears.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Streamlines over the airfoil. Above: closely-spaced, fast. Below: widely-spaced, slow. Speed labels: "v_above = 120 m/s" and "v_below = 80 m/s." The curvature of the top surface creates the speed difference.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Bernoulli's equation shown: "P + ½ρv² = constant." Higher speed → lower pressure. The pressure map fills in: high pressure below, low pressure above. The pressure difference creates lift. "FASTER FLOW = LOWER PRESSURE."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Lift force shown as an upward arrow. Drag force shown (opposing motion). Weight (gravity). The balance of forces: lift = weight for level flight. Angle of attack slider shows how increasing AoA increases lift — to a point.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** Angle of attack increased past stall angle. Streamlines above the wing detach — turbulent separation. Lift drops dramatically. "STALL." The plane needs to pitch down to recover.
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:37):** Symmetric airfoil at zero AoA: no lift. Tilt it: lift appears. "THE SHAPE AND ANGLE, TOGETHER, MAKE FLIGHT."
**Beat 6 (0:37–0:40):** Final: a clean streamlined airfoil with lift arrow. "BERNOULLI'S PRINCIPLE. LIFT FROM PRESSURE DIFFERENCE." Final: "IT'S NOT MAGIC. IT'S FLUIDS."

## Physics Concept Teased
Lift arises from the pressure difference between the faster-moving (lower-pressure) air above the wing and the slower-moving (higher-pressure) air below — a direct consequence of Bernoulli's equation applied to incompressible flow.
