## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
The young Earth — 4.5 billion years ago. A smaller planet (Theia, Mars-sized) approaching from the right. "THIS PLANET IS ABOUT TO HIT EARTH." Pause. "AND THAT'S HOW THE MOON WAS BORN."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:12):** Theia approaching at 4 km/s. Gravitational tidal distortion visible on both bodies as they near each other. "4.5 BILLION YEARS AGO."
**Beat 2 (0:12–0:22):** IMPACT. Not a clean collision — a glancing blow. Theia partially merges with Earth, partially vaporizes. An enormous debris disk ejected in a ring around Earth. Slow motion of the debris eruption.
**Beat 3 (0:22–0:32):** The debris disk: millions of fragments orbiting Earth. Gravitational accretion begins immediately. Fragments clumping. Within ~1000 years in the simulation — a Moon-sized body forming from the ring.
**Beat 4 (0:32–0:40):** Earth post-impact: tilted to 23.5° (the impact set the axial tilt). Rotation: much faster than today. The new Moon: close, enormous in the sky, covered in magma.
**Beat 5 (0:40–0:46):** Time-lapse: the Moon moving away from Earth as tidal forces transfer angular momentum. "THE MOON MOVES 3.8 cm AWAY PER YEAR. STILL TODAY."
**Beat 6 (0:46–0:50):** "One cosmic accident. One Moon. One 23.5° tilt. One Earth with seasons." Final: "THE GIANT IMPACT HYPOTHESIS."

## Physics Concept Teased
The Giant Impact Hypothesis explains the Moon's formation through a collision between the young Earth and a Mars-sized body (Theia) — the ejecta formed a debris disk that rapidly accreted into the Moon, while also setting Earth's axial tilt.

## Audio
Before impact: ominous orchestral build. The impact: a massive, deep explosion — not a crack but a rumble that shakes everything. Debris disk forming: a rushing, chaotic sound. Moon forming from the ring: a gradual, settling resonance. Final: quiet, peaceful — the Earth-Moon system at rest.

## Production Notes
Aspect ratio: 9:16. Frame rate: 60fps; slow-motion (10% speed) at the impact moment. SPH or N-body simulation required. Earth: blue-green with ocean/land texture. Theia: smaller, orange-red. The impact geometry must be a glancing blow — not head-on — to produce the observed angular momentum. Debris disk: thousands of particles, glowing. Moon accreting from the ring: visible over a time-lapse.
