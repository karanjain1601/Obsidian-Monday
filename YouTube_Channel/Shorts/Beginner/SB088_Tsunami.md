## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Open ocean. A low, almost invisible wave — barely 1 meter tall, spanning 200 km. A label: "800 km/h." A speed label for context: "THAT'S A PASSENGER JET." The wave is invisible to ships — but it carries enough energy to destroy a city.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** In deep ocean (4000m depth): wave speed = √(g × h) = 200 m/s = 720 km/h. Wavelength: 200 km. Amplitude: 1m. "SHIPS DON'T NOTICE THIS."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Wave approaches coastline. As depth decreases: wave speed slows (v = √gh). As speed decreases: wavelength compresses. Energy is conserved — must be. "AS IT SLOWS, IT GROWS."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** At 100m depth: speed = 31 m/s. Wave now 10m tall. At 10m depth: speed = 10 m/s. Wave now 20m tall. At 1m depth: speed = 3 m/s. Wave: 30m tall. "THE WAVE DIDN'T GROW. IT COMPRESSED."
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:33):** Slow motion of the wave hitting the coast. The wall of water moving forward. "AT 3 m/s, EVEN A 1m WAVE HAS ENORMOUS MOMENTUM." At 30m: catastrophic.
**Beat 5 (0:33–0:38):** "WHY DOES THE SEA RECEDE FIRST?" The trough of the wave arrives before the crest — the sea pulls back. This is the warning. "THE BEACH GOING DRY = RUN."
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** "One earthquake. One wave. From Japan to California in 9 hours." 2011 Japan tsunami data shown. Final: "SHALLOW WATER IS THE DANGER. SPEED KILLS HEIGHT. HEIGHT KILLS."

## Physics Concept Teased
Tsunami wave speed is √(g×h) — it slows drastically as depth decreases. By conservation of energy, the wave's amplitude must increase as its speed decreases, growing from an imperceptible ocean ripple to a catastrophic wall of water at the shore.
