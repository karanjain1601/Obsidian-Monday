## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Two cars crash into a wall at 50 km/h. Left: rigid car (no crumple zone). Right: modern car (crumple zone). Slow motion. The rigid car stops in 0.01 seconds. The crumple car takes 0.15 seconds to stop. "SAME MOMENTUM CHANGE. VERY DIFFERENT FORCES."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Impulse-momentum theorem shown: "F × Δt = Δp." The change in momentum (Δp) is the same for both cars — same mass, same speed change to zero. "Δp IS FIXED."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Rigid car: Δt = 0.01s. Force on the occupant: F = Δp/0.01 = ENORMOUS. Force spike on graph: off the chart. "THE FORCE KILLS."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Crumple car: Δt = 0.15s (15× longer stopping distance). Same Δp. Force: F = Δp/0.15 = 15× smaller. Force on graph: a lower, longer pulse. "SAME CRASH. SURVIVABLE FORCE."
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** Side-by-side force vs time graphs: rigid car (tall, thin spike) vs crumple car (short, long plateau). Area under both curves: equal (both equal Δp). "SAME AREA. DIFFERENT PEAK."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** "The crumple zone doesn't make the crash less powerful. It makes the crash take longer." Deformation zone shown being crushed — each crumple adds time.
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** Engineering annotation: the crumple zone is designed to deform progressively — maximum energy absorption without catastrophic frame failure. "DESIGNED TO FAIL. TO SAVE YOU." Final: "F × Δt = Δp."

## Physics Concept Teased
The impulse-momentum theorem (F × Δt = Δp) means that for the same change in momentum, extending the stopping time reduces the peak force — crumple zones save lives by converting a short violent stop into a longer gentler deceleration.
