# Short: Car Learns a New Track

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A hand-drawn track — squiggly, irregular, clearly drawn by a human with a mouse in real time. Text: "TRACK DRAWN LIVE. CAR HAS NEVER SEEN THIS." Then a car appears at the starting line.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** The car begins driving. LIDAR rays visible — 12 beams extending from the car in all directions, measuring distance to track walls. The car smoothly navigates the first few turns.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** A sharp hairpin corner. LIDAR rays detect the wall. The car brakes, turns, accelerates out. Clean line through the corner. "THE CAR IS READING THE TRACK."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** A series of S-curves. The car threads through, each turn handled with appropriate speed and steering angle. No crashes.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:34):** The final section of the track: a long straight followed by an abrupt end where the human stopped drawing. The car reaches the end and stops. "LAP COMPLETE." Time shown.
**Beat 5 (0:34–0:42):** Cut to training: 100 cars simultaneously driving on different randomly generated tracks — a birds-eye view of 100 simulation instances. "TRAINED ON 10,000 TRACKS. NONE OF THEM THIS ONE."
**Beat 6 (0:42–0:50):** The new track again — car drives it again, faster this time. "IT GENERALIZED. NOT MEMORIZED." Final: "THAT'S THE DIFFERENCE."

## Physics Concept Teased
Reinforcement learning trained on a diverse distribution of track shapes learns a generalizable driving policy — one that works on any track shape, including ones never encountered during training, because it learned the geometry of corners, not specific tracks.
