## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A comet sweeping in a dramatic arc around the Sun. Its tail — two tails, actually — always point directly away from the Sun, regardless of the comet's direction of travel. "THE TAIL DOESN'T FOLLOW THE COMET. IT POINTS AT YOU."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** Comet approaching the Sun. Tail visible: pointing away from the Sun (not trailing behind the comet's motion). Slow motion. "TAIL POINTS: ANTI-SOLAR."
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:16):** Two tails identified: "ION TAIL" (blue, straight, always directly anti-solar — pushed by solar wind). "DUST TAIL" (white/yellow, slightly curved, pushed by radiation pressure).
**Beat 3 (0:16–0:24):** Comet at perihelion (closest to Sun): both tails at maximum. Comet visible in full. The ion tail straight behind, dust tail slightly curved. Sun direction arrow shown.
**Beat 4 (0:24–0:30):** Comet moving AWAY from the Sun. The tails now point toward the Sun — the comet is moving "backward" through its tails. "THE COMET BACKS AWAY FROM THE SUN."
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** Animation showing the tail direction throughout the full orbit: always anti-solar, regardless of the comet's velocity direction. The tail is not a "wake" — it's a push.
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** "The comet's tail is not where it's been. It's where the Sun's light pushed the gas and dust." Final: "SOLAR WIND AND RADIATION PRESSURE. ALWAYS ANTI-SOLAR."

## Physics Concept Teased
Comet tails always point away from the Sun because the ion tail is pushed by solar wind (charged particle stream) and the dust tail is pushed by radiation pressure (photon momentum) — neither follows the direction of comet motion.
