## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A flat orange dye spreading horizontally in all directions from a central source. It looks like paint spilled in water. No fire. Just spreading. Text: "SAME FLUID. SAME CODE." Then a toggle.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** Boussinesq toggle: OFF. Dye spreads laterally — a flat disk growing outward. Looks nothing like fire.
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:14):** "BUOYANCY ON." Toggle activates. Instantly dye rockets upward, narrowing, elongating into an unmistakably flame-like column within one second.
**Beat 3 (0:14–0:22):** The buoyant plume develops fully: bright hot core rising, cooler edges curling back down as surrounding fluid is entrained. Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities forming on the edge.
**Beat 4 (0:22–0:30):** Slow motion: the flame tip curls and splits — individual eddies breaking off. Color mapping: yellow-white core → orange midsection → red-brown billowing edges.
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** Toggle buoyancy OFF. Instantly: flat spreading dye. Toggle ON: fire rises again. Immediate and dramatic every time.
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** Stable fire plume. "FIRE IS JUST HOT FLUID." Subtitle: "One parameter. One force. One transformation."

## Physics Concept Teased
The Boussinesq approximation adds a single buoyancy force term to the Navier-Stokes equations — yet this one term is entirely responsible for fire's upward rise, the characteristic flame shape, and turbulent eddies at the edges.
