# Short: Why a Compass Points North
**Feeds full video:** Standalone beginner short

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Close-up of a classic compass (white dial, red/blue needle) on a dark wood surface. Needle swings back and forth, then snaps to North. Compass lifts, tilts — needle still points North. Rotates 360° — needle locks North every time. Bold text: **"Why? The answer is 3,000 km underground."**

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**0:03** — Earth cutaway builds: outer half is transparent (we see inside). Layers labeled with colored rings: tan crust (thin, 0–35 km), orange mantle (thick, 35–2,900 km), bright red outer core (liquid iron-nickel, 2,900–5,150 km), dark red inner core (solid, 5,150–6,371 km). Each layer labeled in white text with arrows.

**0:08** — Focus on outer core. Molten iron convection currents animate: bright orange swirling blobs (fluid dynamics approximation — 5 large eddies rotating counterclockwise in northern hemisphere). Text: **"Outer core: liquid iron at 5,000 °C — convecting because of Earth's heat."** Temperature readout: **"5,000 °C"** (red, bold).

**0:13** — Magnetic field generation: as the iron eddies move, white curved arrows appear along each eddy representing current flow (right-hand rule notation). A large white bar magnet icon appears inside the core (N pole at geographic south, S pole at geographic north — correctly opposite to geographic). Text: **"Convecting iron + Earth's rotation = dynamo effect."**

**0:19** — Magnetic field lines emerge from the south geographic pole (which is the magnetic north — labeled to avoid confusion), arc through space, and re-enter at the north geographic pole. 12 white field lines, symmetric dipole pattern. They extend into space (to ~5 Earth radii) then loop back. Label: **"Earth's magnetic dipole — like a giant bar magnet."**

**0:24** — Compass needle superimposed on the field line diagram. The red end (labeled "N") of the needle aligns with the local field line direction (pointing toward geographic North). Arrows show the torque on the needle dipole: **"Compass magnet aligns with field — lowest energy state."** Torque equation panel: **"τ = m × B (torque = magnetic moment × field)"** (white, small).

**0:29** — Geographic vs magnetic north comparison: world map overlay (flat, simple outline). Two labeled arrows from the viewer's location: white arrow **"Geographic North (true north)"** and red arrow **"Magnetic North (Ellesmere Island, Canada)"** offset by about 15°. Text: **"Declination: ~15° offset in most of North America."** A small compass rose shows the offset angle.

**0:34** — Time-lapse of magnetic pole drift: animated dots trace the movement of the magnetic north pole over 400 years (1600–2026). The pole wanders from Hudson Bay area northward toward Siberia. Each dot timestamped (1600, 1700, 1800, 1900, 2000, 2026). Label: **"Magnetic north drifts — currently moving 50 km/year toward Siberia."**

**0:40** — Fun fact card (white): **"Compass was invented in China ~200 BCE using magnetite (lodestone), a naturally magnetic iron ore."** Magnetite mineral icon (dark grey rock with field lines radiating from it).

**0:43** — Final text center: **"Compass → needle aligns with Earth's magnetic field → generated by liquid iron 3,000 km below your feet."**

## Physics Concept Teased
Earth's magnetic field is produced by the geodynamo — convecting liquid iron in the outer core, driven by heat and the Coriolis effect of Earth's rotation, generates electric currents that maintain a global dipole magnetic field; compass needles are magnetic dipoles that align with this field to reach their lowest-energy orientation.

## Production Notes
Code complexity: Medium. Renderer: Canvas 2D. Key visual trick: Earth cutaway — draw a full circle for Earth, clip to right half using ctx.beginPath(); ctx.arc(); ctx.clip() so only the right half is visible; then draw concentric rings (crust, mantle, outer core, inner core) as filled arcs at decreasing radii. Convection currents: animate 5 blobs as circular paths with ctx.arc, each moving at different angular speeds (0.3–0.7 rad/s), color-coded warm orange. Field lines: use dipole field line formula r = r₀ * sin²(θ), 12 lines with r₀ from 1.2 to 8.0 Earth radii. Pole drift animation: precompute (lat, lon) positions from historical data (approximate), project to 2D map using equirectangular projection, animate a moving dot. Runtime: ~46 seconds. Gotcha: visually distinguish geographic north (top of screen) from magnetic north (slightly offset dot); label both prominently to avoid beginner confusion — this is the most common source of viewer comments.
