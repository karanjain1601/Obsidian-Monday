# Short: Bike Self-Corrects

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A bicycle moving forward, no rider. It's leaning slightly to the right. Without any input — the front wheel steers slightly right. The bike rights itself. "THE BIKE FIXED ITSELF. NOBODY TOUCHED IT."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Top-down view: bike moving forward. Front wheel: the castor angle and steering geometry shown. When the bike leans right, the front wheel automatically steers right — not because of a rider, but because of geometry.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** The self-steering mechanism explained: the front wheel contacts the ground at a point BEHIND the steering axis projection. "TRAIL: THE DISTANCE THAT SELF-STEERS."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Gyroscopic precession from the spinning wheels: gyroscope effect annotated. Tilting the spinning wheel creates a torque — the wheel precesses, adding to the self-steering. Both effects (trail + gyroscope) shown as arrows.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:34):** Slow motion of the lean-correct cycle: lean right → front wheel steers right → bike curves right → centripetal force pushes CM left → bike upright. "THE PHYSICS DOES IT FOR YOU."
**Beat 5 (0:34–0:40):** A bicycle with zero trail (experimental): it does NOT self-correct. Falls immediately when perturbed. "TRAIL IS ESSENTIAL."
**Beat 6 (0:40–0:45):** Back to normal bike: small perturbation, self-correction, stable motion. "You don't balance a bike by leaning. You balance by steering." Final: "BICYCLE STABILITY IS STILL NOT FULLY UNDERSTOOD."

## Physics Concept Teased
Bicycle stability involves both the geometric caster effect (front wheel trail creates self-steering when tilted) and gyroscopic precession from spinning wheels — but remarkably, neither effect alone is sufficient, and the full theory is surprisingly complex.
