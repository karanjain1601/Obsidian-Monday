# Short: One Photon at a Time

**Feeds full video:** Light Is a Wave and a Particle
**Duration:** ~50 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A completely black screen. A single white dot appears at a random position. Then another. Then another. They appear slowly, one at a time, each in a seemingly random location. Counter: "PHOTONS: 3."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:12):** Dots accumulating slowly — 10, 20, 30 dots. Completely random pattern. No structure visible. A double-slit barrier is faintly visible at the top. "ONE PHOTON AT A TIME."
**Beat 2 (0:12–0:22):** 100 dots. Still looks random. Slightly more dots in certain columns than others — or is it just coincidence? "NO PATTERN YET."
**Beat 3 (0:22–0:32):** 1,000 dots. The pattern is unmistakably emerging: vertical bands of high and low dot density. Bright bands and dark bands alternating. "WAIT." Counter: "1,000."
**Beat 4 (0:32–0:40):** 5,000 dots. Clear interference fringes. Bright maxima and dark minima perfectly placed. Counter: "5,000." "INTERFERENCE FRINGES."
**Beat 5 (0:40–0:46):** 10,000 dots. A perfect double-slit interference pattern, built one photon at a time. "10,000 PHOTONS. EACH WENT THROUGH ALONE." The fringes match the wave prediction exactly.
**Beat 6 (0:46–0:50):** "Each photon went through both slits. Simultaneously. It interfered with itself." Final: "HOW DOES IT KNOW?"

## Physics Concept Teased
Each photon sent through the double-slit passes through both slits as a wave, then collapses to a single point when detected — but collectively they build up an interference pattern that proves each one experienced both slits simultaneously.

## Audio
Each photon arrival: a single soft click. As they accumulate: the clicks build into a quiet rain, then a steady hiss. At 10,000 photons: silence — the pattern is complete. The audio is purely percussive, like rain on a window.

## Production Notes
Aspect ratio: 9:16. Frame rate: 60fps. Screen: black background. Dots: small, white, semi-transparent with slight overlap building to brightness at maxima. Double-slit: barely visible at top of frame (don't make it the visual focus — the accumulating dots are the story). Counter in corner. The transition from "random" to "pattern" must happen at approximately 500–1000 dots — calibrate the simulation to match this. Time-lapse can be used for 1000→10000 transition.
