## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A glowing glass fiber bending in a 90° curve. Light enters one end — a bright point — travels along the fiber, bouncing at each wall, and exits the other end with the same brightness. A counter: "REFLECTIONS: 0... 1... 2... 3..."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Light ray shown inside the fiber. It hits the glass-air boundary at a shallow angle. Below the critical angle: the ray bounces back inside perfectly. "TOTAL INTERNAL REFLECTION." Reflection counter ticking up.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** The critical angle shown: rays arriving at exactly the critical angle (~42° for glass). Beyond it: some light escapes through the wall. Below it: 100% internal reflection. "CRITICAL ANGLE: ~42°."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:25):** The fiber bends through 90° while light travels inside. Despite the bend, light continues bouncing internally and exits the far end. "THE BEND DOESN'T MATTER IF THE ANGLE STAYS BELOW CRITICAL."
**Beat 4 (0:25–0:30):** Information encoded as pulses: on/off flashing at the fiber's entrance. The same pattern exits at the far end, 100 km away, with minimal loss. "INFORMATION TRAVELS AT LIGHT SPEED."
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** Fiber bundle shown: many fibers together transmitting an entire image. Each fiber carries one pixel. The image: a coherent picture at the other end.
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** "The internet runs on this. Light, bouncing billions of times per second, carrying every message you've ever sent." Final: "TOTAL INTERNAL REFLECTION."

## Physics Concept Teased
When light hits a glass-air boundary at an angle shallower than the critical angle, it undergoes total internal reflection — 100% of the light bounces back — allowing light to travel around bends in a glass fiber with minimal loss.
