# Short: 3 Rules Make a Murmuration

**Feeds full video:** 3 Rules. 10,000 Birds. Nobody Told Them What to Do.
**Duration:** ~55 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
10,000 tiny bird shapes swirling in a massive, fluid murmuration formation against a golden sunset sky. The shape shifts like a living thing. Freeze. "10,000 BIRDS. 3 RULES. NO LEADER."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:55)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:12):** Code snippet flash: Rule 1: "SEPARATION — don't crowd neighbors." Rule 2: "ALIGNMENT — steer toward neighbors' heading." Rule 3: "COHESION — steer toward neighbors' center." That's it.
**Beat 2 (0:12–0:22):** Starting state: 10,000 birds scattered randomly. "3 RULES APPLIED." Within seconds, birds cluster and align — local flocks forming.
**Beat 3 (0:22–0:32):** Large-scale structure emerging. A coherent murmuration shape forming. Waves of turning propagating through the flock as if birds share a nervous system.
**Beat 4 (0:32–0:40):** A PREDATOR appears — a hawk silhouette. The flock SPLITS instantly at the approach, creating a hole around the hawk that moves with it. No individual bird received an instruction.
**Beat 5 (0:40–0:48):** Flock reforms after predator passes. Close-up shows individual birds reacting only to their nearest 7 neighbors.
**Beat 6 (0:48–0:55):** Full murmuration shot. "COMPLEXITY FROM SIMPLICITY." Final: "NOBODY TOLD THEM WHAT TO DO."

## Physics Concept Teased
How do three purely local rules — each bird responding only to its nearest neighbors — produce coherent, large-scale, fluid group behavior that looks like a single coordinated organism?

## Production Notes
Aspect ratio: 9:16. Frame rate: 60fps. Sky: warm golden-hour gradient. Birds: simple arrowhead shapes, 3–4 pixels each, white/dark gray. Flock density creates visual mass. Predator hawk: larger, distinct shape and color. Neighbor radius shown briefly as a glowing circle around one bird. Emergent shapes — whale, hand, wave — are compelling; capture the best formations.
