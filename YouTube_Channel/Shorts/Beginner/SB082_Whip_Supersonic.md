# Short: Whip Crack

**Feeds full video:** How a Whip Goes Supersonic
**Duration:** ~40 seconds

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A whip in slow motion. The handle at rest. The wave traveling down the length. Approaching the tip. Speed counter: "50 m/s... 100 m/s... 200 m/s..." And then — the crack. A shock wave visible in the air. "MACH 1 EXCEEDED."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** The wave travels from handle to tip. As it propagates, the whip narrows. "THE MASS DECREASES TOWARD THE TIP."
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:16):** Energy conservation: the wave carries constant energy (approximately). As mass decreases: "E = ½mv² → if m decreases, v must increase to keep E constant." Velocity counter rising as wave approaches the narrow tip.
**Beat 3 (0:16–0:24):** Mass per unit length graph shown: tapers from thick (handle) to thin (tip). Velocity inversely scales with √(linear mass density). Near the tip: velocity exceeds 343 m/s. "MACH 1."
**Beat 4 (0:24–0:30):** Slow-motion CRACK. The shock wave visible as a cone in air — the Mach cone. "v_tip > 343 m/s. SUPERSONIC." Mach number display: "Mach 1.2."
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** "THE CRACK IS A SONIC BOOM." Same physics as a jet breaking the sound barrier. Small scale, everyday object, same phenomenon.
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** Side-by-side: whip crack (small Mach cone) vs fighter jet (large Mach cone). "SAME PHYSICS. DIFFERENT SCALE." Final: "A WHIP CRACK IS A SONIC BOOM."

## Physics Concept Teased
A whip creates a miniature sonic boom because the wave energy traveling down the tapering whip is concentrated into an ever-decreasing mass per unit length — by conservation of energy, the velocity at the tip exceeds the speed of sound.
