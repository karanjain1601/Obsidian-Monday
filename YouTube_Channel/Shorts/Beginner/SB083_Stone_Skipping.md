## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A stone skipping across calm water. 1 skip. 2 skip. 3 skip. 4. 5. 6. Beautiful, rhythmic, satisfying. Underwater slow-motion of the skip contact: the stone almost hydroplaning on a thin layer of water. "HOW DOES IT WORK?"

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** Stone thrown at 5° (too flat): hits the water and sinks immediately. "TOO FLAT."
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:15):** Stone thrown at 20° (optimal): clean skips. 5 skips shown in slow motion. "OPTIMAL: ~20°."
**Beat 3 (0:15–0:22):** Stone thrown at 45° (too steep): hits nose-first, plunges into water immediately. "TOO STEEP."
**Beat 4 (0:22–0:30):** Physics of the skip: stone hits water at a low angle, the water surface acts as an elastic surface briefly. Lift force shown: perpendicular to the water surface. Duration of contact: ~1 ms. During this 1ms: the stone is "reflected" off the water.
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** Stone spin shown: spin stabilizes the angle of attack (gyroscopic effect). Without spin: stone tumbles, sinks. With spin: it maintains the correct angle for multiple skips. "SPIN IS ESSENTIAL."
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** Skip count vs launch angle graph: sharp peak at ~20°. "THE WINDOW IS NARROW. PHYSICS IS UNFORGIVING." Final: "OPTIMAL: ~20°. SPIN. FLAT STONE."

## Physics Concept Teased
Stone skipping requires the right angle of attack (~20°) for the water surface to generate a hydrodynamic lift impulse that deflects the stone back into the air — combined with spin (gyroscopic stability) to maintain the angle through multiple contacts.
