# Short: Ship Displacement

**Feeds full video:** Why Ships Float
**Duration:** ~40 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A solid steel cube falls into water. It sinks immediately — straight to the bottom. "STEEL SINKS." Then the same steel is formed into a hollow box shape. It floats. "SAME STEEL. FLOATING."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Steel cube submerged at the bottom. Water level unchanged (the cube is small). Weight arrow: "F_gravity = 1000 N ↓." Buoyancy arrow: small, upward. "NOT ENOUGH."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Steel box shape floated at the surface. Water level shows a rise. "VOLUME DISPLACED." Weight arrow: same "1000 N ↓." Now buoyancy arrow: also "1000 N ↑." Balanced.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** The water displaced by the box is shown separately — it's been pushed aside. "DISPLACED WATER WEIGHT = SHIP WEIGHT." A scale shows them equal. Archimedes' principle.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** The box sinks slightly when a weight is placed on it. Sinks more. "MORE DISPLACEMENT = MORE BUOYANCY." The waterline rising with each added weight. The ship finds its new equilibrium.
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:37):** Too much weight added: the box sinks below the rim, water floods in, it sinks. "DISPLACED WATER CANNOT SUPPORT MORE THAN ITS OWN WEIGHT."
**Beat 6 (0:37–0:40):** Aircraft carrier shown: 100,000 tons of steel floating. "100,000 TONNES. AFLOAT." Final: "SHAPE MATTERS MORE THAN MATERIAL."

## Physics Concept Teased
Buoyancy force equals the weight of fluid displaced — by shaping steel into a hollow form, the displaced water volume increases dramatically, generating enough upward force to float the steel even though steel is denser than water.
