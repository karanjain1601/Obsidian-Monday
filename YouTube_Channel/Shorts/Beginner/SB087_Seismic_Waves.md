# Short: P-Wave vs S-Wave

**Feeds full video:** How Seismographs Detect Earthquakes

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A cross-section of the Earth. A red dot marks an earthquake epicenter deep underground. Then — two wavefronts expand simultaneously. One is faster. "P-WAVE: 6 km/s. S-WAVE: 3.5 km/s. THE RACE IS ON."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** P-wave (compression wave) expanding rapidly outward — its ring is wider, faster. S-wave (shear wave) expanding more slowly. The P-wave arrives at a surface station first. Seismograph: first small wiggles. "P ARRIVES FIRST."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** 30 seconds later: S-wave arrives at the same station. Seismograph: larger amplitude wiggles. "S ARRIVES SECOND. LARGER MOTION."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** The time gap between P and S arrivals: "Δt = 45 seconds." Distance formula: "Distance = Δt × (v_P × v_S)/(v_P - v_S) = 360 km." The seismograph is 360 km from the epicenter.
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** Three seismographs at different stations: all receive P then S arrivals at different time gaps. Three circles drawn around each station: the epicenter is at the intersection. "TRIANGULATION."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** Real seismograph trace shown: the P-wave arrival (small, sharp), then the S-wave arrival (larger, later). Time between them: the distance indicator. "THE SEISMOGRAPH IS A RULER IN TIME."
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** "Seismologists knew the epicenter of the 2011 Japan earthquake within minutes. Using only this." Final: "P AND S WAVES. THE EARTH'S OWN TIMING SIGNAL."

## Physics Concept Teased
P-waves (compressional) travel faster through rock than S-waves (shear) — the time difference between their arrivals at a seismograph encodes the distance to the epicenter, allowing triangulation from three stations.
