## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A neutron star rotating rapidly — almost too fast to see. Two beams of radiation sweeping around like a lighthouse. An observer in the distance: each time a beam sweeps past — a bright pulse. "1.4 MILLISECONDS BETWEEN PULSES."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** The neutron star shown: city-sized, 1.4 solar masses, spinning 716 times per second. "FASTER THAN A BLENDER. DENSER THAN AN ATOMIC NUCLEUS." Rotation rate shown.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** The magnetic poles: misaligned with the rotation axis. Radiation beams emanate from the magnetic poles. As the star rotates, the beams sweep around in a cone. "LIKE A LIGHTHOUSE. BUT X-RAYS."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** An observer far away: receives a pulse every time a beam sweeps past. Pulse rate: 1 / (rotation period). Regular as a clock. "PULSE PERIOD: 1.337 ms. CONSISTENT TO 15 DECIMAL PLACES."
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** Timing precision: pulsars are used as gravitational wave detectors (Pulsar Timing Array). The slight timing deviations caused by passing gravitational waves — detectable to nanoseconds. "A COSMIC CLOCK DETECTING RIPPLES IN SPACETIME."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** "If a pulsar is in a binary with another star: the orbital motion Doppler-shifts the pulse timing." Orbital parameter extraction: period, eccentricity, even masses — from timing alone. "TIMING IS EVERYTHING."
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** The pulsar still spinning. "Since 1979, the Hulse-Taylor pulsar has been timed to test general relativity. It has never been wrong." Final: "THE UNIVERSE'S MOST PRECISE CLOCK."

## Physics Concept Teased
A pulsar is a neutron star whose magnetic axis is misaligned with its rotation axis — the radiation beams sweep around like a lighthouse, and the incredibly regular pulse timing (stable to 15 decimal places) makes pulsars the most precise clocks in the universe.
