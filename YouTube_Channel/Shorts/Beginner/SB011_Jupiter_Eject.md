# Short: Jupiter Ejects a Planet

**Feeds full video:** I Built the Solar System in JavaScript. It Was Already Wrong.
**Duration:** ~50 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
The solar system from above, all planets in clean orbital rings. Beautiful, orderly, familiar. Text: "SIMULATION RUNNING. YEAR 0." The year counter begins accelerating.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:12):** Time-lapse. Year counter ticking. Inner planets spiral through fast orbits, outer planets slower. Year 10: everything normal. Year 30: still fine.
**Beat 2 (0:12–0:20):** Year 50. Zoom to the inner system. One orbit very slightly non-circular — a barely perceptible wobble in what should be a perfect ellipse.
**Beat 3 (0:20–0:28):** Year 80. The inner orbit now noticeably precessing and distorting. Jupiter's gravitational influence highlighted with force lines. "JUPITER IS MESSING WITH SOMETHING."
**Beat 4 (0:28–0:36):** Year 95. The perturbed planet's orbit has become radically elliptical — a very long thin ellipse now crossing other orbital lanes. Near-miss with another planet shown in slow motion.
**Beat 5 (0:36–0:44):** Year 100. Close gravitational encounter. Slingshot. The planet's velocity vector flips outward. It spirals out of the frame. "EJECTED."
**Beat 6 (0:44–0:50):** Remaining solar system: calm, as if nothing happened. "ONE ENCOUNTER. ONE PLANET LOST." Final: "THE REAL SOLAR SYSTEM IS LESS STABLE THAN YOU THINK."

## Physics Concept Teased
Jupiter's gravity can cause resonance perturbations that slowly pump energy into a smaller planet's orbit over millions of years, eventually ejecting it from the solar system entirely.
