## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A glass of water with a straight straw in it. The straw appears bent at the water surface — it seems broken. "THE STRAW IS STRAIGHT. SO WHY?"

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** The glass shown from the side. Straw clearly straight (confirmed with a dashed straight line). The perceived straw position: clearly offset from the true position below the water.
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Ray diagram appears. Light from the straw's bottom travels upward through water, hits the surface, BENDS (refracts) as it enters air, then continues to the eye. The eye traces the ray backward in a straight line — seeing the straw at the wrong position.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Snell's law shown: "n₁ sin(θ₁) = n₂ sin(θ₂)." Air: n = 1.00. Water: n = 1.33. "LIGHT SLOWS DOWN IN WATER AND BENDS TOWARD THE NORMAL."
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** The virtual image position shown: the straw bottom appears higher and shifted than its true position. "THE STRAW IS WHERE YOUR EYE THINKS THE LIGHT CAME FROM."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:37):** Bending angle changes with viewing angle — show the straw appearing at different "broken" positions as the viewing angle changes. "THE AMOUNT OF BENDING DEPENDS ON THE ANGLE."
**Beat 6 (0:37–0:40):** "Light bends when it changes medium. Your brain assumes it didn't. The straw pays the price." Final: "REFRACTION. YOUR BRAIN IS WRONG."

## Physics Concept Teased
Refraction occurs when light changes speed at the boundary between two media (air and water) — it bends according to Snell's law. The eye assumes light travels in straight lines, so it traces the refracted ray backward to the wrong position.
