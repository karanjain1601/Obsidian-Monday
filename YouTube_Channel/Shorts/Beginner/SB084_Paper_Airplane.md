## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Two paper airplanes launched simultaneously. One glides beautifully in a long, gentle arc. The other immediately noses down and crashes. "SAME PAPER. SAME THROW. DIFFERENT PHYSICS."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** The gliding airplane: streamlines shown over and under the wing section. Asymmetric flow. Lift force upward, clearly overcoming a portion of gravity. "LIFT > DRAG FOR GLIDING."
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:16):** The crashing airplane: center of mass shown too far FORWARD. Lift center (neutral point) is behind the CM. "UNSTABLE: NOSE PITCHES DOWN." Once pitched down: more negative lift → even more downward → unstable feedback.
**Beat 3 (0:16–0:24):** The stable design: CM is AHEAD of the neutral point (slightly). A small downward pitch is self-correcting — the tail provides restoring force. "CM AHEAD OF NEUTRAL POINT = STABLE."
**Beat 4 (0:24–0:30):** Glide ratio shown: for every 1m drop in altitude, the plane travels X meters forward. "GLIDE RATIO: 10:1." The angle of descent is the glide angle.
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** Adjustments shown: bending the trailing edge up (elevator up) → nose pitches up, slower glide. Down → nose pitches down, faster glide. "FOLD THE BACK EDGE. CHANGE EVERYTHING."
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** The perfectly optimized paper airplane gliding in slow motion. "WEIGHT. LIFT. DRAG. STABILITY. FOUR FORCES. INFINITE DESIGNS." Final: "THE PHYSICS DOESN'T CARE IT'S PAPER."

## Physics Concept Teased
Paper airplane stability requires the center of mass to be ahead of the aerodynamic center (neutral point) — if CM is too far back, any pitch disturbance amplifies into a tumble; if too far forward, the plane is stable but may pitch down.
