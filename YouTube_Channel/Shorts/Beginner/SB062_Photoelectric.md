# Short: Photoelectric Threshold

**Feeds full video:** Why Einstein's Nobel Prize Wasn't for Relativity
**Duration:** ~45 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A metal surface. A bright light shining on it. The light is very bright — but nothing happens. No electrons ejected. "VERY BRIGHT LIGHT. NO ELECTRONS." Then the frequency increases. One electron pops out.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Bright red light (low frequency) blasting the surface. Intensity very high. Electrons counter: 0. "FREQUENCY: 4×10¹⁴ Hz. BRIGHTNESS: MAX. ELECTRONS: 0."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Dim yellow light (higher frequency). Same intensity as classical prediction, but now one electron pops out. Then another. Counter: 3. "FREQUENCY: 5×10¹⁴ Hz. ELECTRONS: 3."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Bright blue light (higher frequency). Many electrons ejecting, moving fast. Counter: 50+. "MORE FREQUENCY = MORE ELECTRONS AND FASTER ELECTRONS."
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** The critical insight: "BRIGHTNESS DOESN'T MATTER. FREQUENCY DOES." Two side-by-side comparisons: dim blue (many electrons) vs bright red (zero electrons). Classical physics predicted brightness should matter — it doesn't.
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** Einstein's explanation: "E = hf." Each photon has energy proportional to frequency. If photon energy < work function: electron stays. If photon energy > work function: electron escapes, kinetic energy = hf - φ.
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** "Einstein explained this in 1905. Not E=mc². This. The photoelectric effect." Final: "EINSTEIN'S NOBEL PRIZE. FOR THIS."

## Physics Concept Teased
Classical wave theory predicts bright light should always eject electrons (given enough intensity), but the photoelectric effect shows only light above a threshold frequency works — revealing that light arrives in discrete energy packets (photons) with energy E = hf.
