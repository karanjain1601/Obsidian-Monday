# Short: Compression Wave

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A row of colored balls representing air molecules, evenly spaced. The leftmost ball is pushed to the right. A compression ripple travels to the right as each ball pushes the next. "THIS IS SOUND."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** The push travels: ball 1 hits ball 2, ball 2 hits ball 3, etc. The compression front moves to the right at the speed of sound. Behind it: the balls return to near-original positions. "COMPRESSION MOVES RIGHT. BALLS BARELY MOVE."
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:16):** Multiple compressions and rarefactions shown. Compression: balls bunched. Rarefaction: balls spread. The pattern repeats. "PRESSURE WAVE. NOT A WATER WAVE."
**Beat 3 (0:16–0:24):** The wave shown abstractly: a pressure graph overlaid. High pressure where balls are bunched (compression). Low pressure where balls are spread (rarefaction). Sinusoidal pressure wave shown.
**Beat 4 (0:24–0:30):** "THE BALLS OSCILLATE AROUND THEIR EQUILIBRIUM. THE WAVE TRAVELS THROUGH THEM." Ball displacement graph shown alongside pressure graph: they're 90° out of phase.
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** Speed of sound shown: 343 m/s in air. "IN STEEL: 5000 m/s. IN WATER: 1500 m/s." Denser medium: tighter spring-like intermolecular bonds → faster wave.
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** "The air didn't travel from the speaker to your ear. The compression did." Final: "SOUND IS A PRESSURE WAVE. NOT MOVING AIR."

## Physics Concept Teased
A sound wave is not moving air — it is a propagating pattern of compressions and rarefactions where individual air molecules oscillate back and forth around their equilibrium positions while the pressure variation travels at the speed of sound.
