# Short: Kepler Equal Areas

**Feeds full video:** Kepler's Three Laws
**Duration:** ~40 seconds

---

## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
An exaggerated elliptical orbit. A planet at perihelion (closest to Sun) moving fast. A timer appears. "1 MONTH." A triangular sector between the planet's position at start and end fills in. "AREA: A."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Planet at perihelion: moving fast, timer runs for 1 month. Sector swept: a narrow but long wedge area. "AREA: A. PERIHELION."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Planet at aphelion (farthest from Sun): moving slowly, timer runs for the same 1 month. Sector swept: a wider but shorter wedge. "AREA: A. APHELION." Both areas shown simultaneously — they're equal.
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:25):** Third equal-time interval at an intermediate position. Swept area: again equal to the other two. All three colored sectors shown simultaneously, clearly equal in area despite different shapes.
**Beat 4 (0:25–0:32):** "WHY?" Speed annotation: fast near the Sun, slow far from the Sun. Angular momentum L = mvr = constant. Near Sun: r small, v large. Far from Sun: r large, v small. L conserved.
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:37):** Formula: "dA/dt = L/2m = constant." The rate of area sweep is constant because angular momentum is conserved.
**Beat 6 (0:37–0:40):** All three sectors side by side. Identical areas. "KEPLER'S SECOND LAW. CONSERVED ANGULAR MOMENTUM. THE PLANET KNOWS NO SHORTCUTS."

## Physics Concept Teased
Kepler's equal-area law is a consequence of angular momentum conservation — since no torque acts along the line from Sun to planet, the areal velocity dA/dt = L/2m remains constant throughout the orbit.

## Audio
The planet's motion generates a musical pitch proportional to its speed. At perihelion: a high, fast tone. At aphelion: a low, slow tone. The pitch changes smoothly throughout the orbit. The equal-time intervals produce the same "area" of sound energy — a sonic metaphor for equal areas.
