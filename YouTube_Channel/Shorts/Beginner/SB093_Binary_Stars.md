## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
Two stars orbiting each other — both the same size. They trace perfect circles around a central point (the center of mass) between them. Symmetrical, hypnotic, beautiful. "TWO SUNS. ONE CENTER."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:45)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:10):** Equal-mass binary: both stars orbit equal circles around the midpoint. Center of mass shown as a fixed red dot. Period labeled. "EQUAL MASS. EQUAL ORBITS."
**Beat 2 (0:10–0:18):** Mass ratio changed: Star 1 = 2× Star 2. Now Star 1 orbits a smaller circle, Star 2 a larger circle. Center of mass is closer to the heavier star. "HEAVIER STAR: SMALLER ORBIT."
**Beat 3 (0:18–0:26):** Extreme mass ratio: Star 1 is 100× the mass of Star 2. Star 1 barely moves — just a tiny wobble. Star 2 orbits in a large ellipse. "THE MASSIVE STAR WOBBLES."
**Beat 4 (0:26–0:32):** "THIS WOBBLE IS HOW WE FIND PLANETS." Animation: a distant star seen from Earth. It wobbles slightly as an invisible planet orbits it. The radial velocity varies. "DOPPLER WOBBLE METHOD."
**Beat 5 (0:32–0:38):** The light from the two stars: as one moves toward us, it's blueshifted. As it moves away: redshifted. The combined spectrum shows periodic Doppler shifts — the spectroscopic binary signature.
**Beat 6 (0:38–0:45):** "60% of all stars are in binary or multiple systems. The Sun is the odd one out." Final: "GRAVITY CREATES PAIRS. PHYSICS LOVES SYMMETRY."

## Physics Concept Teased
Binary stars orbit their common center of mass — the star with greater mass orbits a smaller circle, the lighter star a larger circle, always with the center of mass between them. The wobble of a massive star from a lighter companion is how exoplanets are detected.

## Audio
Two stars: two musical tones (pitches proportional to their masses). As they orbit: the tones Doppler shift — one rising, one falling — creating a periodic beat pattern. The Doppler wobble is audible as a periodic pitch variation.
