## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
A bar of metal in the dark. Temperature: 300K. Room temperature. Invisible. Then a heat source is applied. The metal starts to glow — first invisible infrared (shown as a thermal hue), then dim red, then brighter red.

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:40)
**Beat 1 (0:03–0:08):** Temperature rising: 300K → 700K → 1000K. Metal: dark → dark red → bright red. "1000K: RED HOT."
**Beat 2 (0:08–0:16):** Temperature: 1000K → 1500K → 2000K. Color shifting: red → orange → yellow. "2000K: ORANGE-YELLOW."
**Beat 3 (0:16–0:24):** Temperature: 2000K → 3000K. Color: yellow-white → pure white. All visible wavelengths now emitted with near-equal intensity. "3000K: WHITE HOT."
**Beat 4 (0:24–0:30):** Planck curves shown alongside: at low T, the peak is in IR (right side, invisible). As T rises, the peak moves left into visible range, then into blue. Each temperature's Planck curve shown sequentially.
**Beat 5 (0:30–0:35):** "THE HOTTER THE OBJECT, THE BLUER ITS PEAK." Wien's displacement law: "λ_max = 2898 μm·K / T." Sun surface: 5778K — yellow-white. Cooler stars: red. Hotter stars: blue-white.
**Beat 6 (0:35–0:40):** "The color of a star tells you its temperature." Star color comparison: red star → yellow star (Sun) → blue-white star. "LIGHT IS A THERMOMETER." Final: "BLACKBODY RADIATION."

## Physics Concept Teased
Every hot object emits electromagnetic radiation with a characteristic spectrum determined entirely by its temperature — the Planck blackbody spectrum — and as temperature increases, the peak wavelength shifts from infrared through red to blue, changing the visible color.
