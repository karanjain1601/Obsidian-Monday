## Visual Hook (First 3 Seconds)
The solar interior shown as a cross-section (right half). Near the tachocline (r = 0.71 R_☉, shown as a gold dashed line), a horizontal toroidal magnetic flux tube (bright red/orange, B = 10⁵ G) becomes buoyantly unstable. An Omega-loop rises through the convection zone (shown as a glowing arc of orange field lines erupting to the surface). Two sunspots appear at the surface: "Bipolar magnetic region, 11-year cycle."

## Main Visual Sequence (0:03–0:50)
**0:03–0:10** — The αΩ dynamo mechanism displayed as two panels. Left (Omega effect): differential rotation at the tachocline winds a poloidal field (field lines going north-south) into a toroidal field (east-west rings). A poloidal field line is shown (gold) being stretched and wound into a toroidal tube by the shear flow (white arrows showing fast equatorial rotation). Right (alpha effect): rising convective parcels with Coriolis twisting convert toroidal → poloidal field.

**0:10–0:18** — Flux tube storage and instability: the toroidal flux tube (B = 10⁵ G = 10 T, pressure B²/8π ≈ 4×10⁵ Pa) is stored at the tachocline (stable stratification). When B exceeds B_crit = 10⁵ G, the magnetic buoyancy force F_b = −(B²/8π)∂lnρ/∂r > gravity → tube rises. The magnetic pressure exceeds gas pressure by β = P_gas/P_mag = 0.5 — shown as a bar chart.

**0:18–0:26** — The flux tube trajectory: solved using the thin flux tube approximation: ρ(d²r/dt²) = F_b + F_tension + F_Coriolis. The tube starts at r = 0.71 R_☉, latitude = 30°, and rises to the surface in t = 3 months. The trajectory is an arc that bends eastward due to the Coriolis force (Joy's law tilt). The tilt angle γ = 4° (at 30° latitude) is labeled.

**0:26–0:34** — Sunspot formation: where the Omega-loop intersects the solar surface, two spots of opposite polarity form. The leading spot (positive, blue) and trailing spot (negative, red) are shown. The penumbra and umbra are visible in cross-section — dark cool umbra (T = 3700 K) surrounded by filamentary penumbra. The magnetic field at the spot center: B_spot = 3000 G (0.3 T), shown as arrow density.

**0:34–0:42** — The 11-year cycle: a time-latitude diagram (butterfly diagram) is shown. Sunspot latitude vs year (1870–2026). Each solar cycle: spots start at ±35° latitude, drift equatorward to ±5° by cycle end (the butterfly wings). The polarity reverses each cycle (shown by blue/red coloring: cycle 24 = blue lead, cycle 25 = red lead). At cycle maximum: "Sunspot number = 214 (cycle 25, 2025)."

**0:42–0:50** — The global dynamo: the Babcock-Leighton mechanism — differential flux tube tilt (Joy's law) means trailing polarity flux diffuses poleward and cancels the existing polar cap. After 5.5 years: polar cap reverses. This reversal drives the next cycle. Shown as a sequence of global field maps (GONG synoptic maps): polar field reversing over half a cycle (5.5 yr). Fade to CodedLaws logo.

## Physics Concept Teased
The solar dynamo converts differential rotation (Omega effect) and convective helicity (alpha effect) to regenerate the Sun's magnetic field on an 11-year cycle. Magnetic flux tubes become buoyantly unstable when B > 10⁵ G and erupt as Omega-loops to the surface, creating bipolar sunspot pairs that drive the Babcock-Leighton dynamo.
