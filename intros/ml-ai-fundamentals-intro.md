# Machine Learning & AI Fundamentals: Introduction to All Topics

This document is a guided tour of the 10 sections in the Machine Learning & AI Fundamentals knowledge base — a production-focused reference for engineers building, training, and deploying modern ML systems. The content targets practitioners who need both the mathematical foundations and the applied engineering, and covers everything from linear algebra through deep learning, NLP, computer vision, reinforcement learning, and production MLOps.

**Suggested learning path:** Math Foundations → Classical ML → Deep Learning → NLP → Computer Vision → Reinforcement Learning → MLOps & Production → Advanced Topics → Model Zoo → Research Papers

## 01. Math Foundations

The five mathematical pillars under every model — from a linear classifier to a 100B-parameter transformer. Every later section assumes fluency here; build it first.

**What's covered:**
- **Linear Algebra & Calculus / Autodiff** — Symmetric eigendecomposition A = QΛQᵀ = Σᵢ λᵢqᵢqᵢᵀ (tr A = Σλᵢ, det A = ∏λᵢ); SVD A = UΣVᵀ with the Eckart-Young best rank-k truncation Aₖ = Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ σᵢuᵢvᵢᵀ (error Σᵢ₌ₖ₊₁ σᵢ²) — the basis for PCA and LoRA (ΔW = BA); condition number κ = σ_max/σ_min, with the warning that forming XᵀX squares it (κ(XᵀX) = κ(X)²) so prefer SVD least-squares to the normal equations (XᵀX)β = Xᵀy; Cholesky Σ = LLᵀ to sample correlated Gaussians x = μ + Lz; backprop = reverse-mode autodiff applying the multivariate chain rule (one backward pass yields every gradient); softmax+cross-entropy gradient collapses to ŷ − y; Hessian curvature (H≻0 → min; saddles dominate in high dimensions) and high κ(H) motivate Adam's per-parameter scaling.
- **Probability, Statistics & Optimization** — Bayes P(θ|D) ∝ P(D|θ)P(θ); MLE θ̂ = argmax Σ log p(xᵢ|θ) (Gaussian σ̂² biased by (n−1)/n); MAP adds a log-prior → Gaussian prior = L2/ridge, Laplace prior = L1/LASSO/sparsity; Beta-Binomial conjugacy (posterior Beta(α+h, β+t)); CLT √n(X̄−μ) →d N(0,σ²); convexity (every local min is global), Jensen f(E[X]) ≤ E[f(X)]; GD rates O(1/T) convex and O(κ log 1/ε) strongly convex with step η ≤ 1/L; SGD gradient noise as implicit regularization + the linear LR-scaling rule; momentum (Polyak heavy-ball, Nesterov O(1/T²)); Adam with bias-corrected m̂/v̂, β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999, ε=10⁻⁸; AdamW decoupled weight decay; cosine annealing + linear warmup (mandatory for transformers — without it Adam's v̂≈0 at init diverges).
- **Information Theory** — Shannon entropy H(X) = −Σ p log p (max log|X| at uniform); KL divergence D_KL(p‖q) = Σ p log(p/q) ≥ 0 and asymmetric (forward = mode-covering → VAE encoder; reverse = mode-seeking → RLHF policy); cross-entropy H(p,q) = H(p) + D_KL(p‖q), so minimizing CE ≡ minimizing KL — the information-theoretic justification for CE as the canonical classification loss; perplexity = exp(H); mutual information I(X;Y) = H(X) − H(X|Y) for feature selection and the Information Bottleneck; ELBO = E_q[log p(x|z)] − D_KL(q‖p(z)) with the closed-form Gaussian KL ½(μ²+σ²−ln σ²−1) in every VAE (posterior collapse when KL→0).

**Key mental models:** Linear algebra is the substrate (every forward pass is matrix multiplies; SVD underlies PCA, LoRA, and dimensionality reduction), calculus is the training engine (backprop is just the chain rule), probability is the modeling language (MLE/MAP unify losses with regularizers), and information theory names the objectives (cross-entropy, KL, ELBO). Prefer SVD-based numerics to normal equations, and never train a transformer without LR warmup.

## 02. Classical Machine Learning

The algorithms behind most real-world ML deployments. Understanding them rigorously — not as sklearn calls — is what tells you when deep learning is overkill and how to debug failures.

**What's covered:**
- **Learning theory, linear & probabilistic models** — Bias-variance-noise decomposition E[(f̂−y)²] = Bias² + Var + σ²; PAC bound m ≥ (1/ε)(ln|H| + ln(1/δ)) and VC dimension (linear classifiers in ℝᵈ = d+1); OLS normal equations w* = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy, Gauss-Markov (OLS is BLUE); Ridge (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹Xᵀy (Gaussian prior, always invertible), LASSO L1 (Laplace prior, exact zeros via coordinate descent), Elastic Net; logistic regression σ(wᵀx) with convex BCE whose gradient Xᵀ(p̂−y) mirrors OLS; Naive Bayes P(y|x) ∝ P(y)∏P(xⱼ|y) with Laplace smoothing (Gaussian/Multinomial/Bernoulli; generative, wrong-but-well-ranked); KNN (Cover-Hart 1-NN error ≤ 2R*, curse of dimensionality flattens distances, HNSW for ANN).
- **Trees, ensembles & SVMs** — CART binary splits by Gini 1−Σpₖ² / entropy / variance reduction, cost-complexity pruning (ccp_alpha); ensemble variance Var = ρσ² + (1−ρ)σ²/M (bagging cuts correlation ρ, boosting cuts bias); Random Forest (m=√p features, free OOB error from the ~36.8% = e⁻¹ held out); AdaBoost αₘ = ½ln((1−ε)/ε) (exponential loss); Gradient Boosting on pseudo-residuals; XGBoost second-order Taylor with optimal leaf weight w*_j = −G_j/(H_j+λ); LightGBM GOSS + EFB (~20× faster); SVM max-margin 2/‖w‖, dual with KKT (only support vectors matter), soft-margin ½‖w‖² + CΣξ = hinge loss, kernel trick k(xᵢ,xⱼ) valid by Mercer (RBF exp(−γ‖x−z‖²)).
- **Unsupervised learning & evaluation** — K-Means (minimize WCSS via Lloyd's), K-Means++ D² seeding (E[WCSS] ≤ 8(ln K+2)·OPT), DBSCAN (ε, minPts → arbitrary shapes + explicit outliers), GMM/EM soft responsibilities (K-Means = isotropic hard-assignment GMM), model selection by silhouette / BIC; PCA (eigenvectors of covariance, SVD-preferred, EVR = σᵢ²/Σσ²), LDA Fisher criterion (K−1 directions), t-SNE (minimize D_KL(P‖Q), perplexity 5–50), UMAP (faster, better global structure, transforms new points), Johnson-Lindenstrauss k = O(ε⁻² log N); metrics precision/recall/F1, MCC (best single number for imbalance), ROC-AUC vs PR-AUC, stratified/nested CV, calibration (Platt/isotonic), random search ≈ grid (Bergstra-Bengio: 60 iters).

**Key mental models:** Bias-variance is the master tradeoff — regularize (L1/L2, pruning, ensembles) to swap a little bias for much lower variance. Reach for gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost/LightGBM) on tabular data before neural nets. On imbalanced data use PR-AUC and MCC, never raw accuracy, and guard religiously against leakage (fit scalers/encoders on train only; walk-forward split for time series).

## 03. Deep Learning

Layered networks that learn hierarchical features end-to-end via gradient descent on differentiable computation graphs. Depth buys exponential expressivity over width; the craft is making very deep nets trainable and stable.

**What's covered:**
- **Training core — backprop, activations, losses, optimizers, regularization, normalization** — Universal Approximation (a 2-layer MLP approximates any continuous f, but depth gives wᴸ expressivity for polynomial width); variance-preserving init — Xavier Var(W)=2/(n_in+n_out) for tanh, He Var(W)=2/n_in for ReLU; vanishing gradients (sigmoid σ′≤0.25 → 0.25¹⁰≈10⁻⁶) fixed by ReLU/residuals/normalization, exploding gradients by clipping ĝ = g·max_norm/‖g‖ (1.0 transformers, 5.0 RNNs); activations ReLU → GELU (x·Φ(x), default in BERT/GPT) → SwiGLU (SiLU(xW₁)⊙xW₂·W₃, LLaMA/PaLM FFN, ~9% better loss); losses MSE/MAE/Huber, BCE and categorical CE (gradient ŷ−y), label smoothing ε=0.1, focal loss −α(1−pₜ)^γ log pₜ (γ=2) for imbalance, triplet and NT-Xent; optimizers SGD+momentum (effective LR η/(1−μ)), Adam/AdamW, Lion (sign-based, ⅔ the memory); regularization dropout (inverted, p=0.5 FC), MixUp/CutMix, stochastic depth, SAM (min over ‖ε‖≤ρ, ρ=0.05), SWA; normalization BatchNorm (fails at batch=1), LayerNorm (transformer standard), RMSNorm (no mean-centering, LLaMA/Mistral, 10–15% faster), and Pre-LN vs Post-LN (Pre-LN keeps the residual gradient path clean → required for large models; DeepNorm scales to 1000+ layers).
- **Sequence & convolutional architectures** — CNNs: output ⌊(H+2p−f)/s⌋+1, depthwise-separable conv (~8–9× cheaper, MobileNet), ResNet skip y = F(x)+x (gradient always keeps a +1 path), bottleneck blocks, DenseNet concatenation, EfficientNet compound scaling (α·β²·γ²≈2), ConvNeXt (7×7 depthwise + LayerNorm + GELU). RNNs: BPTT vanishing/exploding, LSTM cell Cₜ = fₜ⊙Cₜ₋₁ + iₜ⊙C̃ₜ (additive path; forget-bias init = 1), GRU (¾ the params), Bahdanau attention breaking the fixed-context bottleneck, beam search with length penalty. Transformers: scaled dot-product Attn(Q,K,V) = softmax(QKᵀ/√d_k)V (the √d_k prevents softmax saturation), multi-head (d_k = d_model/h), causal masking, positional encoding sinusoidal → RoPE (rotate Q,K by position; relative + long-context) and ALiBi (linear distance bias), FFN d_ff = 4·d_model, MoE (Mixtral 8×7B routes top-2 of 8 → 13B active / 47B total), FlashAttention (O(n) memory tiling, 2–4×), GQA/MQA KV-cache reduction.
- **Generative & graph models** — VAE ELBO with the reparameterization trick z = μ + σ⊙ε and closed-form KL; β-VAE disentanglement; VQ-VAE discrete codebook + straight-through estimator. GAN minimax min_G max_D E[log D(x)] + E[log(1−D(G(z)))], optimal D* = p_data/(p_data+p_G) minimizing JSD, with mode collapse / vanishing-gradient failure modes fixed by non-saturating loss, WGAN-GP, spectral norm, StyleGAN2. Diffusion DDPM q(xₜ|x₀) = N(√ᾱₜ·x₀, (1−ᾱₜ)I) trained by ε-prediction ‖ε − ε_θ‖². GNNs: GCN H⁽ˡ⁺¹⁾ = σ(ÂH⁽ˡ⁾W) with Â = D̃^{−½}ÃD̃^{−½} (oversmoothing past 2–3 layers), inductive GraphSAGE neighbor sampling, GAT attention weights, GIN sum-aggregation (as powerful as the Weisfeiler-Lehman test).

**Key mental models:** Depth needs three enablers to train — residual connections (gradient highways), normalization (LayerNorm/RMSNorm with Pre-LN placement), and adaptive optimization (AdamW + warmup). The Transformer displaced recurrence because attention is parallelizable and models long-range dependencies directly; RoPE + RMSNorm + SwiGLU + Pre-LN is the modern decoder recipe. Residual + normalization is what actually makes "deep" possible.

## 04. Natural Language Processing

Algorithms that process, understand, and generate human language — transformed by neural LMs from word embeddings to RNN seq2seq to the Transformer and the LLM era. The dominant paradigm is pretrain → fine-tune → prompt.

**What's covered:**
- **Text representation, seq2seq & metrics** — Tokenization BPE (Sennrich, GPT/LLaMA; ~1 word ≈ 1.3 tokens; byte-level BPE has no OOV), WordPiece (BERT, merge maximizing P(xy)/P(x)P(y)), SentencePiece (▁, language-agnostic); TF-IDF and BM25 (k₁ TF-saturation, b=0.75 length-norm); embeddings Word2Vec skip-gram with negative sampling (Pₙ ∝ f^0.75, k=5–20, king − man + woman ≈ queen), GloVe (weighted log-co-occurrence factorization), FastText (char n-grams handle OOV), ELMo (biLSTM contextual); seq2seq RNN encoder-decoder (fixed-vector bottleneck) → Bahdanau attention e_ij = vᵀtanh(W s + U h) → Transformer cross-attention; decoding greedy / beam (length penalty α≈0.6) / temperature / top-k / nucleus top-p (p=0.9, T=0.8 for chat); metrics BLEU (clipped n-gram precision + brevity penalty), ROUGE (recall/LCS), perplexity exp(H), BERTScore.
- **Encoders (BERT), decoders (GPT) & scaling laws** — BERT MLM (mask 15%: 80% [MASK] / 10% random / 10% keep) + NSP, base 110M / large 340M, Post-LN + GELU; RoBERTa (drop NSP, dynamic masking, 160GB), DistilBERT (6 layers, 97% GLUE), ALBERT (factorized + cross-layer sharing, SOP), ELECTRA (replaced-token detection, trains on all tokens → 4× efficient), DeBERTa (disentangled content/position attention), Sentence-BERT (siamese for real cosine similarity); GPT causal LM −Σ log P(xₜ|x_<t), GPT-1 117M → GPT-2 1.5B (zero-shot) → GPT-3 175B (in-context learning emerges, absent in GPT-2); scaling Kaplan (scale N fast) overturned by Chinchilla D* ≈ 20 tokens/param (GPT-3 ~8.75× undertrained); emergent abilities ~50–100B (partly a metric artifact).
- **Fine-tuning, RAG & agents** — SFT (loss on assistant tokens only; LIMA: 1K curated examples suffice); RLHF three-stage (Bradley-Terry reward model, PPO with reward r_φ − β·log(π_θ/π_ref), 4 models loaded); DPO closed-form L = −log σ(β log[π_θ(y_w)/π_ref(y_w)] − β log[π_θ(y_l)/π_ref(y_l)]) with no RM/RL, plus IPO/KTO/SimPO; parameter-efficient LoRA W = W₀ + (α/r)BA (r=8–64, 0.1–1% of params, merge = zero overhead), QLoRA (4-bit NF4 + paged optimizers → 65B on one 48GB GPU), DoRA; distributed training (DDP all-reduce, ZeRO/FSDP sharding, tensor + pipeline parallel, BF16 over FP16); RAG P(y|q) = Σ P(d|q)P(y|q,d) — chunk 256–512 with 10–20% overlap, dense + BM25 hybrid merged by RRF (k=60), cross-encoder/ColBERT rerank, HNSW (M=16) / IVF-PQ vector DBs, HyDE / RAPTOR / Self-RAG / CRAG; agents ReAct (Thought→Act→Obs), CoT, self-consistency; RAGAS eval (faithfulness, context precision/recall).

**Key mental models:** The pretrain → fine-tune → prompt paradigm dominates — pretraining builds rich language representations, alignment steers them. Chinchilla says train on ~20 tokens/param, but deliberately overtrain small models for cheaper serving. For adaptation, LoRA/QLoRA make fine-tuning cheap and DPO makes preference learning simple; choose RAG for fresh/proprietary facts that need attribution, and fine-tuning for style, format, or latency-critical paths.

## 05. Computer Vision

Teaching machines to interpret images and video. The arc runs from hand-crafted features through deep CNNs (2012) to Vision Transformers (2020) and promptable foundation models (CLIP, SAM, LLaVA).

**What's covered:**
- **Image fundamentals & classical features** — Representation HWC vs CHW, luminance L = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B, HSV/LAB color spaces; 2D convolution (I∗K)[i,j], Sobel/Laplacian kernels, separable Gaussian O(K²)→O(2K); Canny 5-step (smooth → gradient → non-max suppression → double threshold T_low=0.4·T_high → hysteresis); quality metrics PSNR = 10·log₁₀(MAX²/MSE) (>40 dB excellent), SSIM (luminance×contrast×structure), LPIPS (VGG feature L2, perceptual), FID = ‖μ_r−μ_g‖² + tr(Σ_r+Σ_g−2(Σ_rΣ_g)^½) (Inception 2048-d, ≈2–4 SOTA); keypoints Harris R = det(M) − k·tr(M)² (k=0.04–0.06, rotation- but not scale-invariant), SIFT (DoG scale-space, 128-d descriptor, Lowe ratio test 0.8), HOG (8×8 cells, 9 bins → 3780-d pedestrian), ORB (FAST + rBRIEF 256-bit, Hamming, patent-free); matching RANSAC N = log(1−p)/log(1−(1−e)ˢ); optical flow brightness constancy I_x·u + I_y·v + I_t = 0 (aperture problem, Lucas-Kanade reuses the Harris tensor, RAFT for dense); stereo depth Z = B·f/d.
- **Object detection** — IoU = |A∩B|/|A∪B|, mAP (VOC @0.5, COCO @[.5:.95]); two-stage R-CNN → Fast R-CNN (RoIPool + smooth-L1) → Faster R-CNN (RPN, 9 anchors, IoU>0.7 positive / <0.3 negative) + FPN (Pₖ = Cₖ + upsample(Pₖ₊₁), +8 AP); one-stage SSD (8732 default boxes), YOLO (v1 7×7 grid → v3 Darknet-53 3 scales → v4 CIoU + Mish → v8 anchor-free + Task-Aligned Learning); anchor-free FCOS (centerness) and CenterNet (Gaussian heatmap, no NMS); Transformer DETR (Hungarian bipartite matching, 100 object queries, no anchors/NMS, 500 epochs) → Deformable DETR (K=4 sampled points, 10× faster) → RT-DETR (53.1 AP at 108 FPS); post-processing NMS (suppress IoU>0.45), Soft-NMS, WBF.
- **Segmentation & vision transformers / VLMs** — Tasks semantic (per-pixel) / instance (masks) / panoptic (things + stuff), mIoU, PQ = SQ×DQ; FCN (1×1 convs + skip fusion), U-Net (skip concatenation + Dice loss 1 − 2Σpg/(Σp+Σg)), DeepLab (dilated/atrous conv, ASPP rates {6,12,18,24}, v3+ 89% VOC), SegFormer (hierarchical MiT + MLP-only decoder), Mask R-CNN (RoIAlign bilinear → 2× mask AP), SOLO, Mask2Former (masked attention, 100 queries → unified 57.8 PQ); ViT (16×16 patches + [CLS] + 1D pos embeddings, O(N²), needs JFT-300M, ViT-H/14 88.5%), DeiT (distillation token, ImageNet-1K only), Swin (shifted windows → linear cost + hierarchy, Swin-V2-G 90.2%), MAE (75% masking, asymmetric encoder-decoder), DINO/DINOv2 (self-distillation + centering/sharpening, emergent segmentation, ViT-g 86.3% linear probe), SAM (heavy ViT-H encoder once + lightweight decoder per prompt, SA-1B = 1B masks); CLIP (symmetric NT-Xent over WIT-400M, 76.2% zero-shot ≈ supervised ResNet-50), SigLIP (sigmoid loss, batch-independent); Stable Diffusion (latent 8× VAE, CLIP cross-attention U-Net, CFG w=7.5, ControlNet zero-init); LVLMs LLaVA (MLP bridge), BLIP-2 (Q-Former 32 queries), Flamingo (Perceiver Resampler + gated cross-attention).

**Key mental models:** CNNs carry built-in inductive bias (locality, translation equivariance) so they win at moderate data; ViTs have none, so they need JFT-scale pretraining or self-supervision (MAE/DINO) but then scale higher (Swin-V2-G 90.2%). The field's trajectory is hand-crafted features → learned features → promptable foundation models. Two representative leaps: RoIAlign's bilinear sampling (vs quantized pooling) doubled mask accuracy, and DETR removed anchors and NMS by casting detection as set prediction with Hungarian matching.

## 06. Reinforcement Learning

The framework for sequential decision-making: an agent maximizes long-term discounted return through the observe→act→reward loop. Now powers game-playing agents, robot control, and LLM alignment (RLHF).

**What's covered:**
- **MDP fundamentals & dynamic programming** — MDP 5-tuple (S, A, P, R, γ) with the Markov property; discounted return Gₜ = Σ γᵏrₜ₊ₖ, effective horizon H = 1/(1−γ); value functions V^π, Q^π, and advantage A^π = Q^π − V^π; Bellman expectation V^π(s) = Σ_a π(a|s)Σ_s′ P(s′|s,a)[R + γV^π(s′)] and optimality V*(s) = max_a Σ_s′ P[R + γV*(s′)], a γ-contraction (Banach fixed point); exploration ε-greedy (decay εₜ = max(ε_min, ε₀e^{−λt})), UCB1 μ̂ + c√(ln t / Nₜ) with c=√2 and O(√(KT ln T)) regret, Thompson sampling (Beta posterior), intrinsic motivation (count-based, RND, ICM); DP methods policy evaluation, the policy improvement theorem, policy iteration (finite convergence), value iteration, all instances of Generalized Policy Iteration; curse of dimensionality |S| = nᵈ motivates function approximation → deep RL.
- **Model-free methods (MC, TD, Q-learning)** — Monte Carlo (unbiased, high variance, needs full episodes) vs TD(0) bootstrap with error δₜ = rₜ₊₁ + γV(sₜ₊₁) − V(sₜ) (biased, low variance, online); SARSA on-policy (uses the actual next action → safe near cliffs) vs Q-learning off-policy Q ← Q + α[r + γ max_a′ Q(s′,a′) − Q] (Watkins convergence w.p. 1 under Robbins-Monro); Expected SARSA (lower variance); n-step returns and TD(λ) with eligibility traces Eₜ(s) = γλEₜ₋₁(s) + 𝟙[sₜ=s] interpolating MC↔TD; practical rule — Q-learning for tabular control, SARSA when training-time safety matters.
- **Policy gradients & deep RL** — Policy-gradient theorem ∇J = E[∇log π_θ(a|s)·Q^π] via the log-derivative trick; REINFORCE with baseline b(s)=V(s) → advantage; actor-critic (TD-error as advantage), A3C/A2C; TRPO (KL-constrained monotonic improvement) → PPO clipped surrogate min(rₜÂₜ, clip(rₜ, 1±ε)Âₜ) with ε=0.2, GAE(γ,λ) Âₜ = Σ(γλ)ˡδₜ₊ˡ, λ=0.95, γ=0.99, lr 3e-4; DQN (experience replay N=10⁶ + target network every C=10⁴ steps, 49 Atari games), Double DQN (decouples selection/eval to kill overestimation E[max]≥max E), Dueling (Q = V + A − mean A), PER (priority |δ|^α + IS correction), Rainbow (6 tricks, 223% vs 79% median human-normalized, C51 51 atoms, Noisy Nets); continuous control DDPG (deterministic PG + OU noise + Polyak τ=0.005), TD3 (twin clipped-double-Q min, delayed policy d=2, target smoothing), SAC (max-entropy, automatic α-tuning — most sample-efficient); model-based Dyna, DreamerV3 (RSSM latent imagination), MuZero (learned dynamics + MCTS); LLM alignment RLHF (Bradley-Terry reward, KL penalty β=0.02–0.1), DPO (closed-form, no reward model), GRPO (group-normalized Âᵢ = (rᵢ − mean)/std, halves memory — DeepSeek-R1/V3).

**Key mental models:** Everything is Generalized Policy Iteration — alternate evaluation and greedy improvement toward the unique fixed point (V*, π*). Choose an algorithm along the bias-variance axis (MC unbiased/noisy, TD biased/stable) and the on-vs-off-policy axis (SARSA safe, Q-learning optimal). PPO is the default (clipping approximates TRPO's trust region cheaply); for LLMs, DPO and GRPO strip away the reward model / value network that make PPO expensive.

## 07. MLOps & Production

The discipline of deploying, serving, monitoring, and continuously retraining ML systems. A model that only works in a notebook is worthless — production demands reproducible data, low-latency serving, and drift detection.

**What's covered:**
- **Data & feature engineering** — Batch ETL/ELT vs streaming (Kafka topics/partitions, Flink exactly-once with watermarks, tumbling/sliding/session windows), Lambda vs Kappa architecture; orchestration (Airflow DAGs/operators/sensors, Prefect, asset-oriented Dagster); versioning DVC (pointer files + dvc.yaml stages) and LakeFS; feature stores (Feast dual offline/online store, ~1–5 ms Redis online) to prevent training-serving skew (which can cost 5–30% accuracy); validation (Great Expectations, Pandera schema-as-code) + Avro schema evolution; transforms z=(x−μ)/σ, robust scaling by IQR, Box-Cox/Yeo-Johnson, cyclical sin/cos(2πt/T); categorical encoding (one-hot for |V|≤20, target/mean encoding with k-fold to avoid leakage, entity embeddings, hashing trick); selection by mutual information, LASSO L1, and SHAP (TreeSHAP O(TLD²); efficiency/symmetry/dummy axioms), Boruta shadow features; missingness MCAR/MAR/MNAR with MICE imputation; imbalance via class weights wₖ = N/(K·Nₖ) and SMOTE x̃ = xᵢ + λ(x_nn − xᵢ).
- **Experiment tracking & deployment** — MLflow (Tracking/Projects/Models/Registry, Staging→Production, champion-vs-challenger) and W&B sweeps; HPO with Optuna's TPE sampler (models p(x|y<y*)/p(x|y≥y*), maximizes expected improvement, ~2–5× fewer trials than random) + MedianPruner and NSGA-II multi-objective; reproducibility (seed control costs 5–30% GPU perf, git-SHA + environment capture); serialization ONNX (2–5× faster CPU) / TorchScript / TensorRT (2–10×, FP16); optimization quantization FP32→INT8 (4× smaller, 2–4× faster) via dynamic/PTQ/QAT, pruning, knowledge distillation L = α·CE + (1−α)·T²·D_KL (T=2–8, soft targets carry inter-class structure); serving FastAPI, Triton dynamic batching, vLLM PagedAttention + continuous batching (2–24× throughput); Docker multi-stage + K8s Deployment/HPA; rollout via shadow → canary (5%→100%) → blue-green with a two-proportion z-test.
- **Monitoring & drift** — Failure taxonomy: data drift ΔP(X), the more dangerous concept drift ΔP(Y|X) (sudden/gradual/recurring/incremental), the most common acute cause (upstream schema/ETL changes), plus feedback loops and label delay (30–90 days); detection PSI = Σ(p_a − p_e)·ln(p_a/p_e) (<0.1 stable, 0.1–0.25 moderate, ≥0.25 retrain), KS D = sup|F₁−F₂|, chi-squared, MMD (kernel — best for embeddings), online ADWIN (Hoeffding bound) and DDM; tooling Evidently, NannyML CBPE (estimates AUC without labels during the label-delay window), Alibi Detect, Prometheus/Grafana (histogram_quantile p99); retraining full/incremental/triggered/online with active learning (uncertainty/margin/entropy sampling).

**Key mental models:** Define each feature once and serve it to both training and inference — training-serving skew is the silent accuracy killer, and the feature store exists to eliminate it. In production you rarely have ground truth in real time, so you monitor input and score distributions (PSI/KS) and estimate performance label-free (CBPE); PSI > 0.2 plus an estimated-AUC drop is the standard retrain trigger. Ship behind shadow/canary gates so a bad model never sees full traffic.

## 08. Advanced Topics

Frontier research areas defining the current state of the art — each attacks a fundamental limitation of ordinary supervised learning (expensive labels, few examples, single modality, unstable generation, wrong objective, no actions).

**What's covered:**
- **Self-supervised & meta-learning** — Contrastive SSL on the InfoNCE loss −log[exp(sim(zᵢ,zⱼ)/τ) / Σ exp(sim/τ)] (a lower bound on mutual information): SimCLR (NT-Xent, τ=0.5, huge batches 4096–8192, projection head discarded downstream), MoCo (momentum encoder EMA m=0.999 + K=65536 queue); non-contrastive methods that avoid negatives — BYOL (asymmetric predictor + stop-gradient + EMA target), SimSiam (stop-gradient as EM), Barlow Twins (cross-correlation → identity); masked modeling MAE (75% masking, asymmetric encoder-decoder, ViT-H fine-tune 87.8%) and BEiT; DINO/DINOv2 self-distillation (centering + sharpening τ_t=0.04, emergent segmentation, ViT-g/14 86.5% linear probe). Meta-learning N-way K-shot: metric methods (Prototypical Networks — class prototype = mean embedding, nearest by squared-Euclidean; Matching/Relation Nets) and optimization methods MAML (inner θ′ = θ − α∇L, outer meta-gradient through the inner loop, second-order Hessian), FOMAML/Reptile (first-order approximations); in-context learning reframed as meta-learning (transformers implement gradient descent in the forward pass).
- **Multimodal & diffusion** — CLIP contrastive image-text alignment (symmetric InfoNCE over 400M WIT pairs, 76.2% zero-shot ImageNet ≈ supervised ResNet-50); fusion strategies (early/late/cross-modal attention with Q from one modality, K/V from another); token compression (BLIP-2 Q-Former 32 queries trains only 188M bridging frozen ViT-G + LLM; Flamingo Perceiver Resampler → 64 tokens + gated cross-attention tanh(α) init 0); LLaVA (a single MLP connecting CLIP to Vicuna). Diffusion forward q(xₜ|x₀) = N(√ᾱₜ·x₀, (1−ᾱₜ)I) trained by ε-prediction ‖ε − ε_θ‖²; linear vs cosine (s=0.008) noise schedules; samplers DDPM (1000 steps) → DDIM (deterministic 50) → DPM-Solver++ (10) → Consistency (1–4); classifier-free guidance ε̃ = ε_∅ + w(ε_c − ε_∅), w≈7.5; latent diffusion / Stable Diffusion (8× VAE compression → 48× cheaper, CLIP cross-attention U-Net); Flow Matching straight-line OT paths (FLUX, MM-DiT).
- **Alignment/safety & agentic systems** — The alignment problem (reward hacking / Goodhart's Law, inner vs outer alignment, mesa-optimizers, instrumental convergence); RLHF pipeline SFT → Bradley-Terry reward model → PPO with KL penalty β·D_KL(π_θ‖π_ref); Constitutional AI / RLAIF; DPO closed form removing the reward model; mechanistic interpretability (probing, induction heads, superposition, sparse autoencoders L = ‖x−x̂‖² + λ‖f‖₁, activation steering); hallucination taxonomy and mitigation (RAG, self-consistency, chain-of-verification); scalable oversight (debate, process reward models). Agentic systems: the observe→think→act loop, tool/function calling (parallel calls), reasoning CoT / self-consistency / Tree-of-Thoughts / ReAct / PAL; memory tiers (working/episodic/procedural, MemGPT); frameworks LangGraph, LlamaIndex, AutoGen, CrewAI; multi-agent orchestrator+specialists and critic-revision; agent safety (prompt injection — treat tool output as untrusted data, minimal privilege, human-in-the-loop on irreversible actions); benchmarks SWE-Bench, GAIA, WebArena.

**Key mental models:** The unifying move is deriving supervision from structure instead of labels — a pretext/contrastive/masked/denoising objective replaces human annotation. Contrastive methods need many negatives; BYOL/SimSiam show that asymmetry + stop-gradient prevents collapse without them. Diffusion beat GANs by trading one unstable adversarial step for many stable denoising steps (then distilling the steps away). Alignment is two problems: specify the right objective (outer) and verify the model actually pursues it (inner); DPO/RLAIF make preference learning cheaper, and agents raise the stakes because their actions are irreversible.

## 10. Research Papers

From Rosenblatt's Perceptron (1958) to OpenAI's o3 (2025) — the landmark papers that shaped modern AI, plus the meta-skill of reading and evaluating new ones critically.

**What's covered:**
- **How to read papers (the meta-skill)** — Keshav's three-pass method: pass 1 (5–10 min title/abstract/headings → the Five Cs: Category, Context, Correctness, Contributions, Clarity), pass 2 (~1 hr full text, annotate every figure and table), pass 3 (4–5 hr virtual re-implementation, re-derive every equation) — reading front-to-back first is the classic mistake; critical-reading red flags (no public code, cherry-picked benchmarks, test-in-train contamination, no ablation table — ablations are the core, accuracy gains from 10× compute rather than the algorithm); paper scoring (novelty / soundness / clarity / significance, 1–5); tools Zotero, Semantic Scholar (citation graph + auto-TLDR), Connected Papers, Papers With Code, OpenReview (calibrate against public peer reviews); venues NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR, CVPR/ICCV, ACL/EMNLP; spaced-repetition retention at 1/7/30 days.
- **Foundational & deep-learning papers** — Perceptron (1958, convergence in ≤ R²/γ² if separable), Backprop (1986, O(N) gradients), UAT (1989), SVM/C-SVM (1995, kernel trick + soft margin, dominant 1995–2012), No Free Lunch (1997), AdaBoost (1997), LASSO (1996), Random Forests (2001, Var = ρσ² + (1−ρ)σ²/B), XGBoost (2016, 17/29 Kaggle wins), LightGBM (2017, GOSS + EFB); AlexNet (2012, 15.3% vs 26.2% top-5), Dropout, BatchNorm (2015, 10× larger LR), Adam (2014) / AdamW (2019), ResNet (2015, +1 gradient path, 3.57% top-5), VAE (2013), GAN (2014), Transformer (2017, "Attention Is All You Need"), EfficientNet (2019), DDPM (2020, FID 3.17), ViT (2020, needs JFT-300M), Swin (2021), FlashAttention (2022, IO-aware O(N) memory, 2–4×).
- **NLP/LLM, vision/RL & 2023–2025 frontier** — Word2Vec/GloVe/ELMo, BERT/RoBERTa, GPT-2/3 (in-context learning emergent at 175B), T5, Chinchilla (D* ≈ 20N), InstructGPT (1.3B preferred over 175B base via RLHF), LLaMA, DPO, LoRA; SIFT/HOG, Faster R-CNN/FPN/DETR, DINO/MAE/SAM; Q-learning/DQN (replay + target network), A3C, PPO (ε=0.2, GAE λ=0.95), SAC, AlphaGo Zero (self-play + MCTS UCB), MuZero (learned latent dynamics); frontier Mamba (selective SSM, O(1) inference), RWKV, Mixtral/Phi (MoE, data-quality scaling), LLaVA/BLIP-2/Gemini (multimodal), GPT-4 / Claude 3 (Constitutional AI, 200K context), DiT/FLUX (Transformer diffusion), DeepSeek-R1 (GRPO + RLVR, ≈450K USD, MIT) and o1/o3 (hidden CoT; o3: 96.7% AIME, 87.5% ARC-AGI at human level).

**Key mental models:** Read in three passes (never front-to-back on the first), and treat the ablation table as the paper's real contribution — it isolates what actually matters. Reproducibility red flags (no code, no ablations, compute-driven gains) should lower your confidence regardless of the headline numbers.

## Cross-Cutting Mental Models

These principles thread through every section and recur in both interview and production-design contexts:

1. **Pretrain → fine-tune → prompt/align is the universal modern recipe** — Self-supervised or large-scale pretraining builds general representations; a small amount of task or preference data (SFT, RLHF, DPO, LoRA) then steers them. The same pattern appears in NLP (BERT/GPT), vision (MAE/DINO/CLIP), and even RL alignment.

2. **Scale plus the right objective unlocks emergence, but efficiency now beats brute scale** — Chinchilla's ~20 tokens/param, emergent in-context learning at 175B, and inference-time reasoning (o1/R1) show scale matters; MoE (13B active of 47B), MLA (94% KV-cache reduction), quantization, and distillation show that architecture and serving efficiency matter just as much.

3. **Residual connections + normalization + adaptive optimization are what make "deep" trainable** — The skip connection's +1 gradient path (ResNet), LayerNorm/RMSNorm with Pre-LN placement, and AdamW-with-warmup recur in every deep architecture from CNNs to transformers. Without them, gradients vanish or explode.

4. **The bias-variance tradeoff and leakage discipline govern generalization** — Regularize (L1/L2, dropout, ensembles) to trade a little bias for much lower variance, and validate honestly (stratified/nested CV, walk-forward for time series, no fitting on test data). An optimistic offline metric produced by leakage is worthless in production.

5. **In production, distribution shift is inevitable — monitor and gate** — Models decay as P(X) and P(Y|X) drift; detect with PSI/KS/MMD, estimate performance label-free (CBPE) during label delay, and ship behind shadow/canary gates with an automatic retrain trigger. A model that cannot be served, monitored, and retrained has no business value.
